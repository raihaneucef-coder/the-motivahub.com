01 · The Case

Why home training wins at the margin

A gym costs 45 min round-trip + changing + showering. That's 90 minutes overhead for 40 minutes of work. Most people quit not because the workout was hard, but because the activation energy was too high.

Home training removes the commute. The mat is already rolled out. The shoes are by the door. You start 2 minutes after deciding. That's the entire competitive advantage.

The consistency math: 3 gym sessions/week × 90 min overhead = 4.5 hours lost to logistics. 5 home sessions × 5 min overhead = 25 minutes. You get 4 extra hours/week to actually train or recover.

What you need: 2 m² of floor, a mat, and a doorframe. Optional: resistance bands (€15), a pull-up bar (€25), and a timer app. That's it. No rack, no bench, no subscription.

02 · The Formula

Consistency beats intensity. Every time.

Move daily (even 10 min)
+ Progress slowly (1 rep more, not 10)
+ Recover deliberately (sleep > volume)
= the athlete who never stops

Most people train like it's a test: destroy themselves Monday, can't walk Tuesday, skip Wednesday, feel guilty Thursday, start over next week. That's the intensity trap.

The home athlete trains like a practice: show up, do the work, leave with something left in the tank. Tomorrow you do one more rep. That's it. That's the whole system.

The 80% rule: never train to failure at home. Stop at 80% capacity. You'll want to come back tomorrow because today didn't punish you. The goal is to never miss twice, not to max out once.
03 · Daily Protocol

The 20-minute home session

No equipment. No plan. Just 5 movements, done in order, 3 rounds:

MoveRepsRestTarget
Bodyweight squats1520sLegs, glutes, core
Push-ups (knees ok)1020sChest, shoulders, triceps
Reverse lunges (alt.)8 each20sBalance, quads, hip flexors
Plank hold30s20sCore stability
Glute bridges1230sPosterior chain

Total: ~20 min. 3 rounds. Rest 60s between rounds.

Progression (the only rule):

  1. Week 1-2: do the numbers above
  2. Week 3-4: add 2 reps per set (not per exercise — per set)
  3. Week 5: swap push-ups → diamond push-ups (harder variation)
  4. Week 6+: add a 4th round, or slow the eccentric (3s down)

05 · Recovery Is Training

The invisible 23 hours

You don't get stronger during the workout. You get stronger during the sleep that follows it. Training is the stimulus; recovery is the adaptation. Miss recovery and you've just taxed yourself for nothing.

The 4 levers:

  • Sleep 7-9 h. Non-negotiable. Track it for 30 days (wearable or paper). If you train hard and sleep 5h, you're building injury debt.
  • Protein at every meal. 20-30g per sitting. Muscle repair needs amino acids available, not stored.
  • Hydrate 2-2.5 L. Fascia, joints, and cognition all depend on water. Dehydrated tissue = injured tissue.
  • Walk 20 min daily. Active recovery > passive rest. Blood flow clears metabolites without taxing the nervous system.

06 · Cold Exposure Protocol

The 30-second shower finisher

Cold water isn't about suffering. It's about teaching your nervous system that the alarm bell (noradrenaline spike) doesn't require panic. 30 seconds daily builds the same tolerance that Wim Hof markets as 3-hour ice baths.

Progression (4 weeks):

WeekDurationTempFocus
115 sCool (not cold)Breathe slowly, don't gasp
230 sColdExhale longer than inhale
345 sColdest tapStay after the urge to stop
460 sColdest tapCount backwards from 60
When: end of every shower. The anchor is "turn off the hot tap" → "turn cold" → "60 seconds" → "turn off". Habit stack in the body.
07 · Cardio Without the Pavement

Run your own race: pacing discipline

Most people start running too fast, get injured by week 3, and quit. The fix is boring: run slower than you think you need to.

Week 1-4: Walk-Run method

  • 3× per week, outdoor, flat route
  • 3 min brisk walk → 1 min jog → repeat 6 times = 24 min total
  • The jog is SLOWER than you think. You should be able to talk in full sentences
  • Week 2: 2 min jog. Week 3: 3 min. Week 4: 5 min

Week 5+: continuous jog

  • 20 min continuous at "conversational pace" (nose breathing possible)
  • Once/week only. The other 2 days stay bodyweight + walk
  • Never 3 days in a row. Joints adapt slower than muscles

08 · Environment Design

Make the workout the path of least resistance

You don't need motivation. You need your shoes to already be by the door and your mat to already be unrolled. Environment is the invisible coach.

Setup checklist:

  • Mat stays unrolled (or in a visible corner, never in a closet)
  • Workout clothes laid out the night before (on the chair, not in a drawer)
  • Shoes by the front door, not in the shoe rack
  • Resistance bands hang on the door handle (visual trigger)
  • Phone timer app on home screen (not buried in a folder)
  • Water bottle filled and next to the mat
The 2-minute rule applied: if it takes more than 2 minutes to start your workout (find the mat, change clothes, figure out the exercise), you won't do it on a bad day. Pre-set everything so the only action between "I should train" and "I'm training" is stepping onto the mat.
