01 · Welcome

Why the kitchen is the first discipline

You don't fail your goals because you're lazy. You fail because you skipped lunch, ran on sugar, and by 6 PM your prefrontal cortex went offline. Food is the infrastructure under every other habit.

This kit gives you three things:

  1. A 2-hour Sunday protocol that removes every weekday food decision.
  2. A plate framework that stabilizes energy without counting macros.
  3. A friction-removed kitchen where the right choice is the easiest choice.
Rules. No diet language. No "clean eating" moralizing. You're not fixing a character flaw — you're fixing a decision architecture. Every meal is one less decision you don't make at 1 PM when you're already depleted.
02 · The Formula

Kitchen Discipline = Prep × Proximity × Pattern

PREP once (Sunday, 2h)
+ PROXIMITY (right food visible)
+ PATTERN (eat on time, not on emotion)
= zero daily food decisions

Prep means: when hunger hits, the answer already exists in a container with your name on it.

Proximity means: fruit is at eye level; snacks are in a closed cupboard. The 20-second rule — if grabbing it takes more than 20 seconds, you won't choose it when tired.

Pattern means: three meals, fixed windows. Not "when I feel like it" (that's emotional eating wearing a schedule costume). Not "whenever the meeting ends" (that's cortisol choosing your menu).

Book that started this thinking: Michael Pollan, In Defense of Food — amazon.fr/dp/0143114966
03 · The 2-Hour Sunday

Meal Prep: the protocol

Every Sunday, 2 hours. Not cooking — assembling. Here's the exact sequence:

0:00–0:10 · Preheat oven 200°C. Boil a pot for eggs and grains.
0:10–0:35 · Sheet pan: 4 protein portions (chicken thighs, salmon, or tofu). Salt, pepper, oil. Into the oven.
0:20–0:45 · Pot: rice or quinoa (3 portions). Same water, different timing.
0:35–1:00 · Chop raw veg: peppers, broccoli, carrots. Store in glass containers with a damp paper towel.
0:45–1:10 · 8 eggs → boiled, peeled, in a sealed container. Snack insurance.
1:00–1:30 · Assemble 5 lunch boxes: grain + protein + raw veg + sauce (on the side).
1:30–2:00 · Clean as you go. Label containers. Fridge layout: lunches at eye level, snacks behind.
The one rule: if it's not in a container with a label, it doesn't exist. "I'll figure it out Wednesday" = you'll order delivery Wednesday.

04 · Protein First

Protein is not just for athletes

Most people eat carbs first (bread, rice, pasta), protein last. Result: glucose spike → crash → hungry again in 90 minutes → snack cycle.

The order that fixes it:

  1. Vegetables first (fiber coats the stomach, slows absorption)
  2. Protein second (satiety signal starts 15 min before you finish)
  3. Carbs last (by now you're 60% full; portion self-regulates)

05 · Hydration Protocol

Water before willpower

By the time you feel thirsty, you're already 1-2% dehydrated. At 2%: cognitive performance drops, irritability rises, and you reach for sugar instead of water. Most "afternoon slumps" are hydration failures, not discipline failures.

The protocol:

  • 500 ml immediately upon waking (before coffee, before phone)
  • 1 glass with every meal (3× daily = 750 ml extra)
  • 1 bottle at your desk, refilled twice (1 L during work)
  • Last glass 2 hours before bed (protect sleep, not hydration)

Total: 2–2.5 L/day minimum. More if you train, if it's hot, or if you eat high-protein.

Anchor trick: place a full glass bottle next to the coffee maker the night before. The morning coffee anchor now triggers a water habit first. One glass before the first sip of coffee.

06 · The Plate Framework

The plate that keeps you steady

No calorie counting. No macros. Just a visual rule every meal:

Zone% of plateExamples
Vegetables50%Any color, raw or cooked, no starch
Protein25%Chicken, fish, eggs, tofu, legumes
Complex carbs25%Rice, quinoa, sweet potato, whole wheat
FatThumb-sizeOlive oil, avocado, nuts, butter

Why it works: you stop deciding "what to eat" and start deciding "how to fill the zones." The plate is the system. Your brain just executes.

Emotional eating fix: before opening the fridge, drink a glass of water and wait 10 minutes. If you're still hungry, eat. If the urge passed, it was boredom or stress — not hunger.
07 · Fasting: The Honest Version

Intermittent fasting without the cult

IF is not magic. It's a tool that works when it simplifies decisions. It fails when it becomes another thing to white-knuckle.

When IF helps:

  • You naturally skip breakfast and don't feel deprived
  • You want fewer meal decisions (2 meals > 3 + snack)
  • You've tried 16:8 for 2 weeks and it feels easier, not harder

When IF hurts:

  • You binge at the eating window (restriction → reward cycle)
  • You train fasted and performance drops 20%+
  • You're using it to punish yesterday's eating
The discipline version: eat within a consistent 10-hour window (e.g., 8 AM – 6 PM). No food outside. That's it. No 16:8 heroics. The pattern matters more than the protocol.
08 · Fuel Timing

Fuel before willpower

Decision fatigue is glucose depletion. Every choice you make — what to wear, what to answer, what to work on — draws from the same tank. If you haven't eaten, the tank is empty by 3 PM.

The 3-2-1 rule:

  1. 3 meals at roughly fixed times (± 45 min). Not 2, not 5. Three.
  2. 2 snacks max if training or the gap between meals exceeds 5 hours.
  3. 1 thing you never do: make an important decision hungry. Eat first. Then decide.
Practical anchor: set a silent alarm at 12:30 and 19:00. Not "lunchtime" — a specific time. The alarm is the anchor; eating is the stack. This removes the "I'll eat when I finish this email" lie that becomes "it's 4 PM and I had crackers."
