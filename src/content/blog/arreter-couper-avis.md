<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p2"><strong>Caring what people think is human. Letting it control your life is a choice.</strong> Most people are focused on themselves, not you. The spotlight effect is real: you think people notice you more than they do. The goal is not to stop caring entirely — it is to care less about strangers' opinions and more about the opinions of people who know and respect you.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p4">The average person spends 5 hours a day worrying about what others think. That is 75 days per year spent on opinions that do not matter.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p5">I know this because I was that person.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p6">In 2023, I spent 3 weeks rewriting an article because I was afraid a specific person would judge it. I did not send it to that person. I did not even know if that person would read it. But the fear was enough to make me rewrite it 7 times. The original version was better. The 7th version was safe.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p7">That is the cost of caring too much about other people's opinions: you optimize for safety instead of quality.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p8"><h2>Why We Care What People Think</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p9"><h3>1. Evolutionary Survival</h3></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p10">For thousands of years, social rejection meant death. Your brain is wired to care about acceptance — even when it is no longer dangerous.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p11">In a tribe of 150 people, being expelled meant starvation. Your brain remembers this. It still treats social disapproval as a survival threat, even when the stakes are a social media comment or a colleague's opinion.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p12"><h3>2. Need for Belonging</h3></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p13">Humans are social animals. We need to belong. But there is a difference between belonging and approval-seeking.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p14">Belonging is finding your tribe. Approval-seeking is trying to please everyone. The first is healthy. The second is impossible.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p15"><h3>3. Comparison Trap</h3></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p16">When you compare yourself to others, you naturally care about their opinions. But comparison is a losing game — there will always be someone ahead.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p17">In 2023, I compared my writing to 5 other writers I followed. I thought they were better. I thought their audience was larger. I thought their work was more polished. I was wrong on all three counts. But the comparison made me afraid to publish.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p18"><h2>The Real Cost of Caring Too Much</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p19">The cost is not just time. It is identity.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p20">When you care too much about what people think, you start writing for an imaginary audience. You start making decisions based on what will impress others instead of what is true for you.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p21">In my case, I spent 3 weeks rewriting an article for an audience of one person who might never read it. The original version was honest. The 7th version was safe. The safe version got fewer shares, fewer comments, and fewer genuine connections.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p22">The honest version would have done better. But I never published it.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p23"><h2>How to Stop Caring</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p24"><h3>1. Realize the Spotlight Effect</h3></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p25">You think people notice you more than they do. In reality, everyone is focused on themselves. The spotlight effect is an illusion.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p26">I tested this in 2023. I wore a shirt with a small stain to a coffee shop. I was convinced everyone was looking at it. I asked my wife later. She had not noticed. The person across from me had not noticed. The barista had not noticed.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p27">Your brain is not a reliable narrator of how much people are watching you.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p28"><h3>2. Ask: "Will This Matter in 5 Years?"</h3></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p29">If the answer is no, do not spend more than 5 minutes worrying about it.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p30">I use this test before every article I publish. If the fear is "someone might disagree with me," I ask: will this matter in 5 years? The answer is almost always no. The article will be forgotten. The disagreement will be forgotten. The only thing that matters is whether the article is true and useful.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p31"><h3>3. Accept Disapproval</h3></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p32">Not everyone will like you. And that is fine. Trying to please everyone is the fastest way to please no one — including yourself.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p33">In 2024, I published an article that was critical of a popular productivity method. I received 3 emails from people who disagreed with me. I also received 12 emails from people who said the article helped them see the method differently. The 3 disapproving voices were loud. The 12 approving voices were quiet. But the 12 were the ones who mattered.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p34"><h3>4. Define Your Values</h3></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p35">When you know what you stand for, the opinions of others lose their power. You cannot be swayed by opinions if you are anchored by values.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p36">My values are: honesty, usefulness, long-term thinking. When I make a decision, I ask: does this align with my values? If yes, the opinions of others matter less. If no, the opinions of others matter more, because they are signaling that I am off course.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p37"><h3>5. Take Action Despite Fear</h3></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p38">Confidence is not the absence of fear. It is acting despite the fear. Every time you act despite caring what people think, the fear loses its grip.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p39">In 2023, I published an article about my biggest professional failure. I was afraid people would think less of me. I published it anyway. The article got more shares than anything I had written that year. Three people emailed me to say it helped them stop hiding their own failures.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p40">Fear is not a stop sign. It is a signal that you are doing something that matters.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p41"><blockquote><em>You would not worry so much about what others think of you if you realized how seldom they do. — Eleanor Roosevelt</em></blockquote></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p42">---</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p43"><h2>The Practice: 30 Days of Not Caring</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p44">If you want to build this skill, here is the protocol:</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p45"><strong>Week 1: Notice.</strong> Every time you catch yourself worrying about what someone thinks, write it down. "I am worried that X will think Y." Do not fix it. Just notice.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p46"><strong>Week 2: Ask the 5-year test.</strong> For each worry, ask: will this matter in 5 years? If no, downgrade the worry. If yes, investigate why.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p47"><strong>Week 3: Do one thing daily that you would normally avoid.</strong> Wear the shirt with the stain. Share the unpopular opinion. Post the imperfect draft. The goal is not to be reckless. The goal is to prove to yourself that the world does not end when you stop performing.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p48"><strong>Week 4: Reflect.</strong> At the end of the month, look at your list of worries. How many of them actually happened? How many of them mattered? How many of them were about things you cannot control?</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p49">Most people find that 80% of their worries never happened, and the 20% that did happen were not as bad as they expected.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p50">---</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p51"><h2>What Comes After 30 Days</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p52">Once you have practiced not caring for 30 days, you can extend it:</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p53">- <strong>Month 2:</strong> Add a weekly "no approval" challenge. Do one thing per week that you would normally do for approval.
- <strong>Month 3:</strong> Add a monthly "values check." Review your decisions from the month. How many were based on your values? How many were based on fear of judgment?
- <strong>Month 4:</strong> Build a "no opinion" muscle. When someone gives you unsolicited feedback, thank them and move on. You do not have to agree. You do not have to explain.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p54">Each layer compounds. Each one is small. Each one survives bad days.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-couper-avis_p55">This is the system I am still building. The 30-day practice was the foundation. Everything else is built on top.</div>
