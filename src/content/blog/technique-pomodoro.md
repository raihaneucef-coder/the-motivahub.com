The Pomodoro Technique was invented by Francesco Cirillo in the 1980s. He named it after the tomato-shaped kitchen timer he used as a student. It is deceptively simple — and it works.

## How It Works

1. Choose one task
2. Set a timer for 25 minutes
3. Work without interruption
4. When the timer rings, take a 5-minute break
5. After 4 Pomodoros, take a 15-30 minute break

That is it. No complicated apps. No elaborate systems. Just 25 minutes of focused work.

## Why It Works

### 1. It Creates Urgency
A 25-minute timer creates a sense of urgency. You are racing against the clock. This focused energy produces more output than 2 hours of unfocused work.

### 2. It Forces Single-Tasking
During a Pomodoro, you work on one thing only. No multitasking. No switching. One task, 25 minutes, complete focus.

### 3. It Prevents Burnout
The breaks are not optional — they are essential. They allow your brain to recover and maintain high performance throughout the day.

### 4. It Makes Starting Easy
"I only have to work for 25 minutes" is much less intimidating than "I have to work for 4 hours." The Pomodoro removes the barrier to starting.

## Advanced Pomodoro

Once you master the basic technique, try these variations:
- **90-minute blocks** for deep, complex work
- **15-minute Pomodoros** for quick tasks
- **Tracking Pomodoros** to measure your daily output

## Real-World Application

- Writing: 4 Pomodoros = 1,000 words
- Studying: 4 Pomodoros = 2 hours of focused learning
- Coding: 4 Pomodoros = a feature completed
- Reading: 4 Pomodoros = 40 pages

> *The Pomodoro Technique teaches you that time is not the enemy. Distraction is.*
