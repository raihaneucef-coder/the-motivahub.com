We tell ourselves friendship needs big blocks of time we do not have. So we wait for the free weekend that never comes, and friendships quietly go hungry.

They do not need a feast. They need small, regular snacks of attention.

**Five minutes is enough to keep a friendship alive.**

Not a long call. A voice note while you walk. Not a deep dinner. A text that says: *I thought of you today because...* Not a grand gesture. A meme that says: *this is so us.*

The habit is embarrassingly small:

- **One reach-out a day.** One person, one line, one minute. Rotate through the people you do not want to lose.
- **Remember one detail.** Their dog's name, their deadline, their mother's test. Ask about it next time. Being remembered is rarer than being praised.
- **Make the next plan before you leave.** "Next Tuesday walk?" beats "we should catch up sometime" every time.

Friendship is not maintained by intensity. It is maintained by frequency. A friend you hear from for five minutes every two weeks feels closer than a friend you see for five hours twice a year.

You do not need more time. You need a smaller definition of what counts.
