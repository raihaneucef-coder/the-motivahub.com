# Batching: The Secret Weapon of Highly Productive People

Every time you switch between different types of tasks, your brain pays a cognitive tax. It takes time to refocus, reorient, and get back into flow. Batching eliminates this tax.

I learned this in 2023, when I was trying to write a book while running a business. Every day was a mess of email, writing, calls, admin, and more email. I felt busy. I was not productive.

Then I tried batching. I grouped similar tasks into blocks. I stopped switching. I started shipping.

## What Is Batching?

Batching means doing all similar tasks at once instead of spreading them throughout the day.

Instead of:
- Check email at 9 AM, then work on project, then check email at 11 AM, then work on project

Do this:
- Check all email from 9:00-9:30
- Work on project from 9:30-12:00

The result is less time lost to context switching and more time in deep work.

In 2023, I tested this for 30 days. I batched my tasks into four blocks:
- **Communication batch:** 9:00-9:30 AM — email, Slack, messages
- **Creative batch:** 9:30 AM-12:30 PM — writing, design, strategy
- **Admin batch:** 2:00-3:00 PM — invoicing, reporting, planning
- **Learning batch:** 4:00-5:00 PM — reading, research, courses

The result: I went from 400 words per day to 1,200 words per day. I recovered 2.5 hours per day from context switching. I felt less busy but more productive.

## Types of Batches

### Communication Batch

Check email 2-3 times per day at set times. Return all phone calls in one block. Respond to messages in a single batch.

In 2023, I checked email at 9:00 AM, 12:00 PM, and 4:00 PM. Three times per day. No exceptions. The rest of the time, email was closed. Slack was closed. Messages were ignored.

The first week was painful. I kept imagining emergencies. Nothing happened. No one died. No one got angry. The world continued without my immediate response.

By week 3, I had reclaimed 1.5 hours per day. That is 7.5 hours per week. 30 hours per month. That is a full week of work recovered from checking email less.

### Creative Batch

Write all your articles in one sitting. Record all your videos in one session. Design all your graphics in one block.

In 2023, I wrote all my articles on Monday mornings. 9:30 AM to 12:30 PM. Three hours of writing. No email. No Slack. No research. Just writing.

The creative batch works because creative work requires a different mental mode than administrative work. Switching between modes is expensive. The creative batch keeps you in creative mode for an extended period, so you do not have to pay the switching tax.

### Administrative Batch

Do all your invoicing at once. File all your documents in one session. Complete all your reports in one block.

In 2023, I did admin on Tuesday afternoons. 2:00-3:00 PM. One hour. Invoices, reports, planning. The admin batch works because admin work is low-cognitive. It does not require deep focus. It just requires time. Batching it into one block prevents it from spreading throughout the week.

### Errand Batch

Do all your shopping in one trip. Run all your errands in one block. Complete all your calls in one session.

In 2023, I did errands on Friday afternoons. 4:00-5:00 PM. Grocery shopping, calls, any other errands. The errand batch works because errands are location-dependent. Doing them in one block saves travel time and mental energy.

## Why Batching Works

### 1. It Eliminates Context Switching

Every time you switch tasks, your brain pays a cognitive tax. Research from the University of Michigan found that multitasking can reduce productivity by up to 40%. The switching cost compounds with every task change.

Batching eliminates this tax by keeping you in the same mental mode for an extended period. You pay the switching cost once, not every few minutes.

### 2. It Creates Deep Work Blocks

Deep work requires extended focus. Batching creates the conditions for deep work by protecting large blocks of uninterrupted time.

In 2023, my creative batch was 3 hours of uninterrupted writing. That is enough time to get into flow, do meaningful work, and produce something valuable. Fragmented time does not allow this.

### 3. It Reduces Decision Fatigue

Every time you switch tasks, you make a new decision: what to do next, how to do it, what to focus on. Batching reduces the number of decisions by keeping the task constant.

In 2023, I did not have to decide what to do every hour. I had a schedule. The schedule was the boss. The schedule does not negotiate.

### 4. It Makes Progress Visible

When you batch similar tasks, you can see your progress in one area. Instead of "I did a little bit of everything," you can say "I completed all my writing for the week" or "I cleared all my email."

Progress is motivating. Visible progress is more motivating. Batching makes progress visible.

## The Batching System I Used

Here is the exact system I used in 2023:

**Monday:**
- 9:00-9:30: Communication batch (email, Slack, messages)
- 9:30-12:30: Creative batch (writing, design, strategy)
- 12:30-2:00: Lunch + rest
- 2:00-3:00: Admin batch (invoicing, reports, planning)
- 3:00-5:00: Learning batch (reading, research, courses)

**Tuesday-Thursday:**
- Same as Monday, but with meetings in the afternoon

**Friday:**
- 9:00-9:30: Communication batch
- 9:30-12:30: Creative batch
- 12:30-5:00: Errand batch (shopping, calls, errands)

The system worked because it was simple, predictable, and protected my creative energy for the tasks that mattered most.

## What I Got Wrong (And How I Fixed It)

I made three big mistakes:

**Mistake 1: Not protecting the creative batch**
At first, I allowed meetings to creep into the creative batch. "Just one 30-minute meeting." Then two. Then three. The creative batch was no longer protected. The writing suffered. The fix: meetings are only on Tuesday-Thursday afternoons. Monday and Friday mornings are sacred creative time.

**Mistake 2: Checking email too often**
At first, I checked email every hour. "Just in case." The constant switching destroyed my focus. The fix: three times per day, 30 minutes each. No exceptions.

**Mistake 3: Not batching admin**
At first, admin spread throughout the week. Invoices on Monday, reports on Wednesday, planning on Friday. The constant context switching drained my energy. The fix: one hour on Tuesday afternoon. All admin in one block.

If you want to test this for yourself, here is the protocol:

1. **Week 1:** Track your current task switching. How many times do you switch tasks per day? How much time do you spend on email?
2. **Week 2:** Implement one batch: communication. Check email 3 times per day, 30 minutes each.
3. **Week 3:** Add a creative batch. Protect 2-3 hours of uninterrupted creative work.
4. **Week 4:** Add an admin batch. One hour, all admin tasks.

Most people who try this for 30 days report:
- 2-3 hours recovered per day
- 2x increase in deep work output
- Lower stress and anxiety
- More progress on important projects

The data will convince you faster than any productivity book.
