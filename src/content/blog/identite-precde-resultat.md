Most people try to change their lives by changing their behavior. They set goals, create plans, and rely on willpower. But the deepest, most lasting change happens when you change who you believe you are.

Your identity precedes your outcomes. If you see yourself as a reader, you will read. If you see yourself as an athlete, you will exercise. If you see yourself as a writer, you will write.

The behavior is just evidence of the identity.

## The Identity-Behavior Loop

Your identity drives your behavior. Your behavior reinforces your identity. This creates a loop:

1. **Identity:** "I am a healthy person"
2. **Behavior:** You eat well and exercise
3. **Reinforcement:** The behavior proves the identity
4. **Strengthening:** The identity becomes stronger
5. **Repeat:** The loop continues

The same loop works in reverse:
1. **Identity:** "I am lazy"
2. **Behavior:** You avoid effort
3. **Reinforcement:** The behavior proves the identity
4. **Strengthening:** The identity becomes stronger
5. **Repeat:** The loop continues

The key is to **start the loop you want** — even before you have evidence for it.

## How to Change Your Identity

### 1. Decide Who You Want to Be

Do not start with goals. Start with identity.

- Not: "I want to lose 10 kg" → "I am a healthy person"
- Not: "I want to write a book" → "I am a writer"
- Not: "I want to get promoted" → "I am a high performer"

The identity is the destination. The goals are the path.

### 2. Prove It With Small Actions

Every action is a vote for your new identity. You do not need a landslide — you just need a majority.

- One page read = one vote for "I am a reader"
- One push-up done = one vote for "I am an athlete"
- One paragraph written = one vote for "I am a writer"

Small actions accumulate. Over time, the evidence becomes overwhelming. The identity solidifies.

### 3. Never Miss Twice

When you miss a day, you cast a vote for the old identity. One vote does not determine the election. But two votes start a trend.

The rule is simple: never miss twice. One missed day is a fluke. Two missed days is a pattern. Keep the pattern pointing toward your new identity.

### 4. Surround Yourself With the Right People

You become the average of the people you spend time with. If you want to be a reader, spend time with readers. If you want to be fit, spend time with fit people. If you want to be successful, spend time with successful people.

Social proof is powerful. When everyone around you has the identity you want, it feels natural to adopt it.

### 5. Celebrate the Identity, Not Just the Outcome

When you read a page, celebrate: "That is what a reader does." When you exercise, celebrate: "That is what an athlete does." When you write, celebrate: "That is what a writer does."

The celebration reinforces the identity. It tells your brain: "This is who I am now."

## The Compound Effect of Identity

One action does not change your identity. But a thousand actions do. Every time you act in alignment with your new identity, you make it more real.

After 30 days of reading, you are a reader. After 30 days of exercising, you are an athlete. After 30 days of writing, you are a writer. The identity is no longer something you are trying to be. It is something you are.

> *You do not have to be great to start, but you have to start to be great. The first step is deciding who you want to become. The second step is proving it with your actions.*
