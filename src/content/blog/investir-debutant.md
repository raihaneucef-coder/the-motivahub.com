<div data-i18n-html="article_investir-debutant_p2">You do not need thousands to start investing. You need consistency. Learn the simplest way to start building wealth from scratch.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_investir-debutant_p3">The best time to start investing was 20 years ago. The second best time is today. Investing is how you make your money work for you — instead of you working for your money.</div>


<div data-i18n-html="article_investir-debutant_p4"><h2>Why You Should Invest</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_investir-debutant_p5">- <strong>Inflation eats savings:</strong> If your money earns 1% in a savings account but inflation is 3%, you are losing 2% per year
- <strong>Compound interest:</strong> The most powerful force in finance. 500€ per month at 8% for 30 years = 745,000€
- <strong>Financial freedom:</strong> Investing is how you build wealth that does not depend on your salary</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_investir-debutant_p6"><h2>How to Start</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_investir-debutant_p7"><h3>1. Build an Emergency Fund First</h3>
Before investing, have <a href="/journal/fond-urgence/">3-6 months of expenses saved</a>. This prevents you from needing to sell investments during an emergency.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_investir-debutant_p8"><h3>2. <a href="/journal/invest-like-beginner/">Start with Index Funds</a></h3>
Index funds are baskets of stocks that track the market. They are diversified, low-cost, and require no expertise. Examples: S&P 500 index fund, Total Market index fund.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_investir-debutant_p9"><h3>3. Invest Regularly</h3>
Set up automatic monthly investments. This is called dollar-cost averaging — you buy more when prices are low and less when prices are high.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_investir-debutant_p10"><h3>4. Keep Costs Low</h3>
Choose funds with low expense ratios (under 0.5%). High fees destroy returns over time.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_investir-debutant_p17">Why the fee line matters more than it looks: an expense ratio is charged every single year, so it compounds against you just as returns compound for you. As a rough, widely used rule of thumb, each extra 1% of annual fees can shave something close to a fifth off a portfolio's final value over a couple of decades versus a near-zero-cost index fund. The fund does not have to underperform for the fee to cost you — the arithmetic is enough.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_investir-debutant_p11"><h3>5. Stay the Course</h3>
The market will go up and down. Do not panic-sell during dips. Long-term investing wins.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_investir-debutant_p12"><h2>Common Mistakes</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_investir-debutant_p13">- Waiting for the "right time" — it does not exist
- Trying to time the market — even professionals fail at this
- Checking your portfolio daily — it creates anxiety
- Investing money you need in the short term — only invest money you can leave for 5+ years</div>
