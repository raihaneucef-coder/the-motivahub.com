<div data-i18n-html="article_histoire-concierge-millionnaire_p3"><h1>The Janitor Who Became a Millionaire: A Story of Discipline</h1>
Ronald Read was a janitor in Vermont. He cleaned floors at JCPenney and pumped gas at a gas station. He drove a used car. He wore safety pins to hold his coat together.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_histoire-concierge-millionnaire_p4">When he died at age 92, he left behind 8 million dollars.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_histoire-concierge-millionnaire_p5"><h2>The Secret</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_histoire-concierge-millionnaire_p6">Read's secret was not complicated. He lived below his means. He saved consistently. He invested in good companies and held them for decades. He never sold in a panic. He never tried to time the market. He just kept buying and holding.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_histoire-concierge-millionnaire_p7"><h2>The Lesson</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_histoire-concierge-millionnaire_p8">Wealth is not about how much you earn. It is about how much you keep and how long you let it grow. Read earned an average salary his entire life. But he lived like he earned half of that and invested the rest.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_histoire-concierge-millionnaire_p9">The math is simple:
- Save 500€ per month
- Invest in index funds returning 8% per year
- After 30 years: 745,000€</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_histoire-concierge-millionnaire_p10">You do not need to be a genius. You need to be patient.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_histoire-concierge-millionnaire_p11"><h2>The Real Wealth</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_histoire-concierge-millionnaire_p12">Read's real wealth was not the money. It was the discipline. The ability to delay gratification, live simply, and stay the course — that is the real wealth. And it is available to anyone willing to practice it.</div>
