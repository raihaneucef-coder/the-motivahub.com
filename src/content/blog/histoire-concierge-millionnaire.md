# The Janitor Who Became a Millionaire: A Story of Discipline
Ronald Read was a janitor in Vermont. He cleaned floors at JCPenney and pumped gas at a gas station. He drove a used car. He wore safety pins to hold his coat together.

When he died at age 92, he left behind 8 million dollars.

## The Secret

Read's secret was not complicated. He lived below his means. He saved consistently. He invested in good companies and held them for decades. He never sold in a panic. He never tried to time the market. He just kept buying and holding.

## The Lesson

Wealth is not about how much you earn. It is about how much you keep and how long you let it grow. Read earned an average salary his entire life. But he lived like he earned half of that and invested the rest.

The math is simple:
- Save 500€ per month
- Invest in index funds returning 8% per year
- After 30 years: 745,000€

You do not need to be a genius. You need to be patient.

## The Real Wealth

Read's real wealth was not the money. It was the discipline. The ability to delay gratification, live simply, and stay the course — that is the real wealth. And it is available to anyone willing to practice it.

> *Wealth consists not in having great possessions, but in having few wants. — Epictetus*
