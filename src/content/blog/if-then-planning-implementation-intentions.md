<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p1"><p>You know the gap. You decide to exercise tomorrow, write the report first thing, stop checking your phone at night. Then tomorrow arrives, and the decision is still there—unmade.</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p2"><p>If-then planning is a small tool for that gap. It looks almost too simple:</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p3"><blockquote>
<p>“If it is 7:00 a.m., then I will put on my shoes and walk for twenty minutes.”</p>
</blockquote></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p4"><p>The sentence has two parts: a cue (when or where) and a response (what you will do). In psychology, this is called an implementation intention. The claim is not that the sentence magically creates motivation. The claim is that it moves the decision from the moment of action to a moment of planning, when you are calmer and can think ahead.</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p5"><h2>The gap between intention and action</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p6"><p>Webb and Sheeran (2006) meta-analyzed 47 experimental tests on intention and behavior — a separate question from whether if-then plans work. Changing intentions had a medium-to-large effect on intention (d = 0.66), but a small-to-medium effect on behavior (d = 0.36). Good intentions are necessary, but they are not sufficient.</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p7"><p>Gollwitzer and Sheeran (2006) looked at 94 tests with 8,461 participants. Implementation intentions had a medium-to-large effect on goal achievement, d = 0.65 (95% CI 0.60–0.70). The effect was d = 0.61 for getting started and d = 0.77 for shielding goal pursuit.</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p8"><p>A newer meta-analysis changes how those numbers should be read. Sheeran, Listrom, and Gollwitzer (2025) synthesized 642 independent tests. Implementation intentions were effective across cognitive, affective, and behavioral outcomes (d = 0.27 to 0.66). The sample-weighted average was d = 0.36. Effects were larger when plans used the contingent if-then format, when participants were highly motivated, and when plans were rehearsed. The review also found substantial publication bias: a robust Bayesian correction yielded a smaller model-averaged estimate, d = 0.15 (95% CI 0.08–0.22). The effect appears robust, but smaller and more heterogeneous than the 2006 figure suggested.</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p9"><p>The two analyses do not cover exactly the same scope: Gollwitzer and Sheeran (2006) focused on implementation intentions and goal achievement, while Sheeran et al. (2025) mapped a broader set of outcomes and plan components. The smaller 2025 estimate is therefore not a simple downgrade of the 2006 result; it is a different synthesis with a wider scope and explicit correction for publication bias.</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p10"><p>That does not mean if-then planning is useless. It means the practical claim should be modest: it can help, especially when the goal matters, the plan is genuinely if-then, and you rehearse it. It is not a guarantee, and the average effect in the broader literature is small. Most of the 2006 tests were experimental and short-term; the 2025 review is broader, but it still does not prove that one sentence keeps a habit alive for years.</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p11"><h2>What an if-then plan actually is</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p12"><p>It is not a goal. It is not a to-do list. It is a link.</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p13"><p>A goal: “I want to get fitter.”<br>A plan: “If it is Monday, Wednesday, or Friday at 7:00 a.m., then I will walk to the corner and back.”</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p14"><p>A good plan names three things:</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p15"><ol>
<li>A cue you will actually notice.</li>
<li>A response you can actually do.</li>
<li>A situation where the response is possible.</li>
</ol></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p16"><p>Gollwitzer (1999) described implementation intentions as delegating action control to environmental cues. The cue does part of the work: when it appears, the response is already decided.</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p17"><h2>How to write one that works</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p18"><h3>1. Choose one goal and one bottleneck</h3></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p19"><p>The 2025 meta-analysis found larger effects when people were highly motivated, so <a href="/journal/the-goal-behind-the-goal/">start with a goal you actually care about</a>. Then pick the moment where you stall. Is it starting? Continuing when you are bored? Resisting a temptation? Choose one.</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p20"><h3>2. Name a concrete cue</h3></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p21"><p>“When I have free time” is not a cue. “When I sit down at my desk after lunch” is. Use a time, a place, an object, an event, or a feeling that reliably shows up.</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p22"><h3>3. Name a small, observable response</h3></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p23"><p>“Eat better” is not observable. “Put a protein source on the plate before anything else” is. Make the first action small enough that the plan does not depend on a heroic mood.</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p24"><h3>4. Write the sentence in if-then form</h3></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p25"><p>“If [cue], then I will [response].”</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p26"><p>Examples:</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p27"><ul>
<li>If I open my laptop in the morning, then I will open the document and write one sentence before checking email.</li>
<li>If I feel the urge to check social media during a deep-work block, then I will put the phone in the drawer and return to the next line.</li>
<li>If it is 9:00 p.m., then I will put my phone on the charger in the kitchen and read ten pages.</li>
<li>If I miss a workout, then I will do the ten-minute version the next day instead of abandoning the week.</li>
</ul></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p28"><h3>5. Rehearse it before you need it</h3></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p29"><p>Read the sentence aloud once. Imagine the cue and the response. Achtziger et al. (2008) found that implementation intentions can help shield goal striving from unwanted thoughts and feelings. The point is to practice the link before the moment gets noisy.</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p30"><h3>6. Test for one week, then adjust</h3></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p31"><p>At the end of the week, ask:</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p32"><ul>
<li>Did the cue actually happen?</li>
<li>Did I notice it?</li>
<li>Was the response possible?</li>
<li>Did the plan fail because of the plan, or because of a skill or resource problem?</li>
</ul></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p33"><p>If the cue never happened, change the cue. If the response was too big, shrink it. If you forgot, add a visual reminder.</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p34"><h2>Common mistakes</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p35"><ul>
<li><strong>Planning too many if-thens at once.</strong> Start with one bottleneck.</li>
<li><strong>Using a vague cue.</strong> “When I get a chance” gives your brain nothing to catch.</li>
<li><strong>Making the response a project.</strong> The response should be the first move, not the whole mountain.</li>
<li><strong>Planning only for good days.</strong> Add a fallback: “If I miss the morning session, then I will do the ten-minute version at lunch.”</li>
<li><strong>Treating the plan as a test of willpower.</strong> The plan is supposed to reduce the number of in-the-moment decisions, not prove you are tough.</li>
</ul></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p36"><h2>What it can and cannot do</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p37"><p>If-then planning is useful when:</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p38"><ul>
<li><a href="/journal/retroengineering-objectifs/">you know what you want to do</a>;</li>
<li>the main problem is starting or staying with it;</li>
<li>you can identify a reliable cue;</li>
<li>the response is within your control.</li>
</ul></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p39"><p>It is not a treatment for ADHD, anxiety, depression, or sleep disorders, and it is not a substitute for professional care. If those are affecting your follow-through, talk to a qualified professional.</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p40"><p>The evidence is also more modest than the early headline number suggested. The 2025 meta-analysis found substantial publication bias and a small corrected average effect. Treat if-then planning as a cheap, useful nudge, not a powerful cure.</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p41"><p>It also does not replace skill. A plan can get you to the piano, but it will not teach you to play. For complex work, pair the plan with instruction, feedback, and deliberate practice.</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p42"><p>And it does not guarantee long-term maintenance. The meta-analytic evidence supports a modest effect, but most studies remain experimental and short-term. Long-term habit change usually needs a <a href="/journal/habit-stacking-routine/">supportive environment</a>, repetition, and review.</p></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p43"><h2>A seven-day trial</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p44"><ol>
<li>Pick one goal.</li>
<li>Write one if-then sentence for the moment you usually stall.</li>
<li>Put the sentence where you will see it.</li>
<li>Rehearse it once in the morning.</li>
<li>Track only two things: did the cue happen, and did you do the response?</li>
<li>At day seven, change one variable: cue, response size, or reminder.</li>
</ol></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_if-then-planning-implementation-intentions_p45"><p>The point is not a perfect sentence. The point is <a href="/journal/process-vs-outcome/">a loop you can inspect and improve</a>.</p></div>
