We treat goals as cages. Once we set one, we feel trapped by it. If we miss a day, we feel guilty. If we change our mind, we feel like we failed.

A better way is to treat a goal as a compass.

A cage says: you must stay inside me. A compass says: this is north — walk that way as best you can today.

**A compass keeps you oriented without demanding perfection.** You can take a detour, you can rest, you can even change the destination when you learn more. The compass does not judge; it just keeps pointing.

This is why the most motivated people are not the most rigid. They are the most clear about direction and the most flexible about the path.

Try it with one goal this week:

- **Write it as a direction, not a contract.** Not "I will run every day at 6am or I fail," but "I am becoming someone who moves daily — north is movement."
- **Check the compass, not the clock.** At night, ask: did I move north today, even a little? One walk, one healthy meal, one page — that is north.
- **Adjust without shame.** If the goal no longer excites you, ask what it was pointing to. Often you keep the direction (health, growth, freedom) and change the vehicle.

Goals that motivate are not the ones that pressure you. They are the ones that orient you — so even on a messy day, you know which way is forward.

Pick one compass for this month. Keep it visible. Let it pull you, not trap you.
