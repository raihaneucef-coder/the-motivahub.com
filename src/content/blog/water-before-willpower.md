faq:
  - q: "Does dehydration affect willpower and focus?"
    a: "Yes. In controlled experiments, mild dehydration of about 1-2% of body mass degraded attention, working memory and mood in young men (Ganio et al., <em>Br J Nutr</em> 2011, <a href=\"https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21736786/\">PubMed 21736786</a>). You also wake up having gone 7-8 hours without water, so a glass before coffee replaces that loss — which removes one candidate cause of mid-morning fog, rather than curing fog itself."
  - q: "How much water should I drink per day?"
    a: "Start from the official benchmark, not a per-kilo formula: EFSA sets adequate total intake at about 2.5 litres a day for men and 2.0 for women, counted across food <em>and</em> drinks (<a href=\"https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2010.1459\">EFSA, 2010</a>). The 35 ml per kg shorthand (about 2.45 litres at 70 kg) lands in the same band but is a coaching rule of thumb, not a guideline. The urine test still works: pale yellow = fine, dark = behind. Sip through the day instead of gulping."
  - q: "Should I drink water first thing in the morning?"
    a: "Yes — before coffee, before food. After 7-8 hours of sleep you are mildly depleted, and 500 ml replaces that. The often-quoted metabolic jump is real but tiny: the original German study measured roughly a 30% rise in metabolic rate for under an hour after 500 ml, much of it just warming the water to body temperature (<a href=\"https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14671205/\">Boschmann et al., 2003</a>), and later independent reassessments found the effect marginal, under 3% (<a href=\"https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26690288/\">Charrière et al., 2015</a>). Drink it to rehydrate and to start the day with one completed decision. Keep a glass by the bed the night before."

<div data-i18n-html="article_water-before-willpower_p2">Before you reach for willpower, reach for water. Hydration is the cheapest performance enhancer you own.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_water-before-willpower_h2a"><h2>Sometimes it is dehydration</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_water-before-willpower_p3">We blame willpower for what is often just dehydration.</div>


<div data-i18n-html="article_water-before-willpower_p4">Headache in the afternoon, hunger an hour after lunch, foggy thinking at 3 PM — before you fix any of these with discipline, fix them with water.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_water-before-willpower_h2b"><h2>Your brain needs water</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_water-before-willpower_p5"><strong>Your <a href="/journal/hydratation-performance/">brain is mostly water</a>. When you are a little dry, it works a little worse.</strong> Not dramatically, just enough to make everything feel harder than it is.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_water-before-willpower_h2c"><h2>What 1 to 2 percent costs you</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_water-before-willpower_p10">"A little dry" has a number attached to it. Losing just 1–2% of body mass in fluid measurably impaired attention, concentration and mood — in the controlled studies, young men working in a warm room (Ganio et al., 2011, <em>British Journal of Nutrition</em>, <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21736786/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PubMed 21736786</a>; summarized in <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4207053/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">this review of the hydration-and-cognition research</a>). That is the size of one long sweat, and it lands squarely on the tasks willpower needs.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_water-before-willpower_p11">A concrete target beats a vague "drink more": Europe's food-safety authority sets adequate total intake at about 2.5 litres a day for men and 2.0 for women, counted across food <em>and</em> drinks together (<a href="https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2010.1459" target="_blank" rel="noopener">EFSA, 2010</a>). Most people who lose the 3 PM battle are quietly several hundred millilitres behind that pace — check the number before blaming the character. For timing and performance detail, see <a href="/journal/hydratation-performance/">hydration and performance</a>.</div>


<div data-i18n-html="article_water-before-willpower_h2d"><h2>Make it easy to drink</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_water-before-willpower_p6">Make it easy to drink:</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_water-before-willpower_p7">- <strong><a href="/journal/fuel-before-willpower/">Start the day with water</a>.</strong> Before coffee, drink a glass. You woke up dry.
- <strong>Keep it visible.</strong> A bottle on the desk is a reminder your brain respects.
- <strong>Drink before you feel thirsty.</strong> Thirst is a late signal, not an early one.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_water-before-willpower_p12">Hydration is also a habit you can borrow. People who drink more water tend to eat better too, although nobody has settled which way the causality runs — it is plausibly the structured routine that carries both. So attach water to a cue you already never miss: a glass the instant the kettle boils, a bottle emptied before you refill your coffee. Do not aim to remember water; aim to inherit it from an existing habit.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_water-before-willpower_p8">You do not need eight glasses perfectly. You need to drink a little more than you do now, consistently.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_water-before-willpower_p9">Water will not solve everything. But it will make solving everything a little easier — and that is enough to keep going.</div>
