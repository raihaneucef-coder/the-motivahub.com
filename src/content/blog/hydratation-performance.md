    a: "Even 2% body water loss reduces endurance by 10-20%, increases perceived effort, and impairs strength output. At 4%, cognitive function drops — reaction time slows and decision-making suffers. Elite athletes lose approximately 2% performance capacity for every 1% dehydration."
  - q: "How much water should I drink during exercise?"
    a: "150-250ml every 15-20 minutes during sustained activity over 60 minutes. For shorter sessions, hydrate before and after. Don't wait until thirsty — thirst signals you're already 1-2% dehydrated. Weigh yourself pre/post session: replace 1.5× any lost weight in fluid."
  - q: "Do electrolytes matter for hydration?"
    a: "Yes, for sessions over 60-90 minutes or heavy sweating. Water alone dilutes blood sodium, risking hyponatremia. A pinch of salt + squeeze of lemon in your bottle, or a commercial electrolyte tab (300-500mg sodium), maintains fluid balance and prevents cramps in prolonged exercise."

<div data-i18n-html="article_hydratation-performance_p2">Losing about 2% of your body mass in fluid measurably costs you endurance and concentration — and the deficit grows with heat and session length. Water is the simplest, cheapest, and most effective <a href="/journal/water-before-willpower/">performance enhancer</a> available.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_hydratation-performance_p3">You can survive 3 weeks without food. You can only survive 3 days without water. Yet most people walk around chronically dehydrated — and wonder why they feel tired, foggy, and unproductive.</div>


<div data-i18n-html="article_hydratation-performance_p4"><h2>How Dehydration Affects You</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_hydratation-performance_p5">- <strong>~2% body-mass loss</strong> — endurance output and concentration start to measurably drop
- <strong>3–5% loss</strong> — the decrements grow, and heat makes them bite sooner
- <strong>Strength and power</strong> — less consistently affected; the tidy "every 2% costs 10% of strength" ladder that circulates online is not what the reviews show</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_hydratation-performance_p6">Even mild dehydration <a href="/journal/gestion-energie/">reduces cognitive function</a>, mood, and energy levels.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_hydratation-performance_p7"><h2>How Much Water Do You Need?</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_hydratation-performance_p8">The general rule is 35ml per kg of body weight. For a 70kg person, that is 2.45 liters per day. But this varies based on activity level, climate, and individual needs.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_hydratation-performance_p9">A simple test: if your urine is pale yellow, you are hydrated. If it is dark yellow, drink more.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_hydratation-performance_p10"><h2>When to Drink</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_hydratation-performance_p11"><h3>1. First Thing in the Morning</h3>
You dehydrate overnight. Drinking 500ml of water first thing rehydrates your body and kickstarts your metabolism.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_hydratation-performance_p12"><h3>2. Before Meals</h3>
Drinking water before meals helps with digestion and can reduce appetite.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_hydratation-performance_p13"><h3>3. During Exercise</h3>
Drink 200-300ml every 15-20 minutes during exercise.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_hydratation-performance_p14"><h3>4. After Exercise</h3>
Replace the fluid you lost. Weigh yourself before and after exercise — drink 1.5ml for every gram lost.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_hydratation-performance_p15"><h2>Tips to Drink More</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_hydratation-performance_p16">1. Carry a water bottle everywhere
2. Set hourly reminders
3. Drink a glass of water before every meal
4. Flavor your water with lemon or cucumber
5. Eat water-rich foods (watermelon, cucumber, oranges)</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_hydratation-performance_p17"><blockquote><em>Water is the driving force of all nature. — Leonardo da Vinci</em></blockquote></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_hydratation-performance_p20">A self-check that needs no equipment: urine pale like lemonade rather than dark like apple juice is the rough, widely used public-health shorthand for "probably hydrated." Concentrated and dark means drink now — and take the rest of the afternoon seriously.
