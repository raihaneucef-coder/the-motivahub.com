<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p2"><strong>Self-sabotage is fear in disguise.</strong> Your brain protects you from risk by creating doubt, distraction, or excuses. Most self-sabotage shows up as procrastination, perfectionism, self-doubt, or choosing comfort over growth — even when you know better. The goal is not to never sabotage yourself again. The goal is to notice it faster, recover sooner, and make the next choice different.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p3"><h1>How to Stop Self-Sabotaging Once and for All</h1></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p4">I missed my own book launch.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p5">Not because I forgot. Not because I was sick. Not because something external happened. I missed it because I spent the three days before the launch scrolling through social media, reorganizing my desk, and cleaning my apartment. I was doing everything except what I knew I needed to do: send the final emails, test the download link, write the launch announcement.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p6">I had spent 8 months writing the book. The launch was the moment. And I sabotaged it.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p7">That was in March 2024. I was sitting in my apartment in Casablanca, watching the launch window close, and I realized: I was not lazy. I was not unmotivated. I was afraid. Afraid that the book would not be good enough. Afraid that people would judge it. Afraid that after all that work, it would not matter.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p8">So I found ways to make sure it would not matter. I created chaos. I created distractions. I created reasons to fail.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p9">This is self-sabotage. And it is more common than you think.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p10"><h2>Why You Sabotage Yourself</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p11">Self-sabotage is not laziness. It is fear in disguise.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p12">Your brain is wired to protect you. When you are about to do something that feels risky — start a business, publish your work, have a difficult conversation — your brain sounds the alarm. It creates doubt, procrastination, distraction, anything to keep you safe.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p13">The problem is that "safe" and "fulfilled" are not the same thing. Your brain confuses comfort with safety. And comfort is where dreams go to die.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p14">In my case, the comfort was scrolling. The safety was not launching. The cost was 8 months of work, wasted because I was afraid to let it be seen.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p15">Common forms of self-sabotage:
- Procrastinating on important work
- Starting projects but never finishing them
- Picking fights before big moments
- Setting goals too low (or too high)
- Negative self-talk before challenging situations
- Seeking validation instead of progress</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p16"><h2>The Identity Behind the Behavior</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p17">Self-sabotage is not about the behavior. It is about the identity behind it.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p18">If you believe you are "not the kind of person who succeeds," you will unconsciously make sure you do not succeed. If you believe you are "bad at relationships," you will find ways to prove it.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p19">The sabotage is not random. It is consistent with the story you tell yourself about who you are.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p20">In my case, the story was: "I am a writer who writes, but I am not a writer who publishes." Publishing felt like a different category. It required something I did not think I had: the courage to be seen.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p21">To stop sabotaging yourself, you need to change the story.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p22"><h2>How to Break the Cycle</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p23"><h3>1. Recognize the Pattern</h3></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p24">Self-sabotage thrives in the dark. When you bring it into the light, it loses power.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p25">Start noticing your triggers:
- When do you procrastinate most? (After a big win? Before a deadline?)
- What emotions precede the sabotage? (Fear? Boredom? Anxiety?)
- What story do you tell yourself? ("I am not ready," "It does not matter anyway")</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p26">Write it down. Patterns become visible when they are documented.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p27">In my case, the pattern was: 3 days before any launch or public moment, I would start cleaning, scrolling, or finding urgent but unimportant tasks. The emotion was fear. The story was "I am not ready."</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p28">Once I saw the pattern, I could interrupt it.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p29"><h3>2. Separate the Behavior from the Identity</h3></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p30">You are not a procrastinator. You are a person who procrastinates. You are not lazy. You are a person who sometimes chooses comfort over effort.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p31">This distinction matters because behaviors can change. Identities feel permanent.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p32">When you catch yourself saying "I always do this," replace it with "I have done this in the past, but I am choosing differently now."</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p33">After my failed launch, I stopped saying "I am a writer who publishes." I started saying "I am a writer who is learning to publish." The "learning" part made it okay to be imperfect. It made the next launch less scary.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p34"><h3>3. Create Friction for Sabotage, Ease for Progress</h3></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p35">Make the bad behavior harder to do:
- Delete social media apps from your phone during launch weeks
- Put your phone in another room during deep work
- Set boundaries with people who drain your energy</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p36">Make the good behavior easier to do:
- Prepare your workspace the night before
- Write the first sentence of your next day's work on a sticky note
- Tell one person about your goal so they can check in</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p37">For my next launch, I did three things:
1. I deleted Instagram and TikTok from my phone 5 days before the launch.
2. I wrote the launch announcement 3 days before and scheduled it.
3. I told my wife: "If I start cleaning the apartment the day before launch, tell me to stop."</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p38">The launch went smoothly. I sent the emails. I tested the link. I posted the announcement. The book sold 127 copies in the first 24 hours.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p39"><h2>What I Got Wrong (And How I Fixed It)</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p40">I tried three variations before landing on the current system.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p41"><strong>Attempt 1: Willpower.</strong> I thought if I just tried harder, I would not sabotage myself. This did not work. Willpower is finite. Sabotage is automatic.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p42"><strong>Attempt 2: Guilt.</strong> I tried to shame myself into not procrastinating. "You are wasting time." "You are lazy." This made the sabotage worse. The guilt created more fear, and the fear created more sabotage.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p43"><strong>Attempt 3: System.</strong> I created a pre-launch checklist. 48 hours before any public moment, I had to complete 5 specific tasks. The checklist removed the decision. I did not have to decide whether to prepare. I just followed the list.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p44">The current version is the smallest one that works. Anything bigger fails on bad days.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p45"><h2>The 3-Step Recovery Protocol</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p46">When you catch yourself sabotaging, do not panic. Here is the protocol:</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p47"><strong>Step 1: Notice.</strong> "I am procrastinating right now." Say it out loud if you can. The act of naming it removes some of its power.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p48"><strong>Step 2: Pause.</strong> Take 3 breaths. Ask: "What am I afraid of?" The answer is usually smaller than you think.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p49"><strong>Step 3: Choose differently.</strong> Do 2 minutes of the thing you are avoiding. Not 2 hours. Not 20 minutes. 2 minutes. The doorway is enough.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p50">This is the protocol I used after my failed launch. I noticed the procrastination. I paused and asked what I was afraid of. I chose differently: I wrote one sentence of the launch announcement. Then I wrote another. By minute 10, I had the full draft.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p51"><h2>When This Habit Breaks (And What to Do)</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p52">It will break. Here is how to recover:</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p53"><strong>Day you forgot:</strong> Don't make up for it. Just do it tomorrow. The streak matters less than the daily practice.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p54"><strong>Day you're traveling:</strong> Hotel rooms are perfect for reflection. Write down what you are avoiding and why. I've done this.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p55"><strong>Day you're sick:</strong> Skip it. The 3 priorities don't matter when you're trying to recover.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p56"><strong>Day you're too busy:</strong> If you can't find 5 minutes to notice your patterns, you need them most. Take 2 minutes. That is enough.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p57"><h2>The Relationship Between Sabotage and Identity</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p58">The deepest part of the system is the identity statement. Without it, the rule is a behavior with no destination. With it, the rule is a vote in an election.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p59">"I am a writer who publishes" is the destination. The 2-minute rule of writing one sentence is the vote. The 2-day rule is the protection of the vote. The hallway is the consequence of the vote. The identity statement is what makes the vote meaningful.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p60">When the system is working, the identity statement becomes more true over time. The statement is no longer aspirational. It is descriptive. You are a writer who publishes. You are a runner who races. You are a reader who finishes books. The votes have made it so.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p61">When the system is not working, the identity statement remains aspirational. You say "I am a writer who publishes" but you have not published in three months. The statement is a wish, not a fact. The votes have not been cast.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p62">Self-sabotage is what happens when the identity statement and the behavior are in conflict. The behavior says "I am not ready." The identity says "I am a writer who publishes." The sabotage is the brain trying to resolve the conflict by making the behavior match the identity.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p63">The fix is to make the identity and the behavior align. You do this by voting. One sentence at a time.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p64"><h2>A Simple Test for Your Week</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p65">If you want to test this for yourself, here is the protocol:</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p66">1. <strong>Pick 5 days this week.</strong>
2. <strong>Each morning, notice when you are avoiding something.</strong>
3. <strong>Write it down: "I am avoiding [X] because I am afraid of [Y].</strong>
4. <strong>Do 2 minutes of the thing you are avoiding.</strong>
5. <strong>Track the data for 2 weeks.</strong>
6. <strong>Decide if the pattern is changing.</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p67">Most people who try this for 2 weeks report:
- More awareness of their triggers
- Less guilt about procrastination
- Faster recovery from setbacks
- More action on important tasks</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p68">The data will convince you faster than any self-help book.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p69"><h2>What Comes After Awareness</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p70">Once you have noticed your patterns, you can build a system:</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p71">- <strong>Week 1:</strong> Notice and name the sabotage. Do not fix it yet. Just see it.
- <strong>Week 2:</strong> Add the 2-minute rule. When you notice sabotage, do 2 minutes of the opposite.
- <strong>Week 3:</strong> Add the identity statement. Change "I am a procrastinator" to "I am someone who takes action."
- <strong>Week 4:</strong> Add the 2-day rule. If you miss, recover the next day. No guilt.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p72">Each layer compounds. Each one is small. Each one survives bad days.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_arreter-auto-sabotage_p73">This is the system I am still building. The awareness was the foundation. Everything else is built on top.</div>
