<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p8"><h2>The first 24 hours after a miss</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p9">The first move is always the same: do the 2-minute version of the habit, today, no matter what.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p10">Not tomorrow. Not in an hour. Now. The 2-minute version is the doorway. The doorway has to be walked through the day after a miss. This is non-negotiable. The 2-minute version is small enough that the negotiation is over before it begins. Two minutes. That's it. The miss is not a verdict on the habit. The 2-minute version after the miss is the verdict on the system.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p11">Most people skip this step. They decide to "restart tomorrow" or "skip the 2-minute version today" because they have a longer session planned for later. This is the most common path from a single miss to a full relapse. The miss becomes a two-day miss. The two-day miss becomes a week. The week becomes a month. The month becomes the day you give up.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p12">The protocol is the opposite: 2 minutes, today, no negotiation.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p13"><h2>Why a longer session is the wrong response</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p14">The temptation after a miss is to do a longer session to "make up for" the missed day. This is wrong. Three reasons.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p15"><strong>First, the longer session is a coping mechanism for the guilt of missing.</strong> The guilt says, "I missed yesterday, so I have to do extra today." The guilt is lying. The extra session is not a recovery. It is a payment for the missed day. The habit does not need payment. The habit needs continuity.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p16"><strong>Second, longer sessions after a miss set up a pattern where missing is rewarded with a longer session.</strong> This is the wrong incentive. The next time you consider missing, your brain will calculate: "If I miss, I do extra next time." Missing becomes strategic. The system breaks.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p17"><strong>Third, the longer session is harder than the 2-minute version.</strong> After a miss, your resistance is at its highest. The harder the session, the more likely the next miss. The smaller the session, the more likely the recovery.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p18">The 2-minute version is the only correct response. It is small on purpose. The smallness is the point.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p19"><h2>The 2-day rule as a hard boundary</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p20">The 2-day rule is the immune system of any habit. It is also the most often ignored. The rule is simple: never miss twice in a row. One miss is a data point. Two is a verdict. The verdict is not "you failed." The verdict is "the system has detected a pattern, and the pattern is a relapse."</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p21">When the 2-day rule is triggered, the response is the same 2-minute version. The session after the second miss is more important than the session after the first. The first miss is survivable. The second is the boundary. The third miss is the failure.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p22">The 2-day rule is a hard boundary. Crossing it is the failure mode of the entire system. But crossing it is not the end of the habit. The recovery is possible. It requires restarting the 2-minute rule from day 1 — which is exactly what you did the first time. The system is the same. The streak is reset. The protocol is the same.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p23"><h2>The three rules</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p24">The protocol for the day after a miss has three rules. Apply them in order.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p25"><strong>Rule 1: Do the 2-minute version, today.</strong> The smallest possible version. No negotiation about the duration. No decision about whether to do it. The decision was made when the habit started. The miss is not a reason to renegotiate.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p26"><strong>Rule 2: Do not negotiate.</strong> Negotiation is where misses become relapses. The negotiation sounds like "I will do it later today" or "I will do a longer session tomorrow to make up for it" or "I will skip the 2-minute version today because I have other things going on." Every version of the negotiation is a path to a second miss. Do not negotiate.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p27"><strong>Rule 3: Apply the 2-day rule tomorrow.</strong> The recovery protocol is the same for every miss — that is the point of a protocol. Tomorrow is a normal day. The 2-minute rule fires as usual. The 2-day rule is no longer triggered. The miss becomes a data point.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p28">These three rules, applied in order, prevent the miss from becoming a relapse. Most people apply none of them. Most people skip the day after a miss. The day after is the day the habit dies.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p29"><h2>What to remember</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p30">A single miss is not the end of a habit. It is the test of the system. The day after the miss is the day the system runs. If the system runs, the habit survives. If the system does not run, the habit dies.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p31">The 2-minute version after a miss is the most important vote you will ever cast. It is the vote that proves the system survives the miss. The miss is not the failure. The skipped recovery is the failure.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p32">The protocol is short. The 2-minute version, today. No negotiation. The 2-day rule tomorrow. The system is mature when the day after a miss is no different from any other day.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_missed-day-protocol_p33">The system is mature when the door is permanent.</div>
