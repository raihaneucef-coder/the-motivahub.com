# Emergency Fund: Why It Is Your Financial Foundation
Your car breaks down. Your roof leaks. You get sick. These are not possibilities — they are certainties. The question is not if they will happen, but when. An emergency fund is what keeps them from becoming financial disasters.

## How Much Do You Need?

The standard recommendation is 3-6 months of essential expenses. For most people, that is:
- **Minimum:** 3 months of rent, food, transportation, utilities
- **Ideal:** 6 months of essential expenses
- **Conservative:** 12 months of essential expenses

## How to Build It

### 1. Start Small
You do not need 10,000€ tomorrow. Start with 500€ — enough to cover a minor emergency. Then keep building.

### 2. Automate It
Set up an automatic transfer of 10-20% of your income to a separate savings account. Treat it like a bill — non-negotiable.

### 3. Keep It Separate
Do not keep your emergency fund in your checking account. Put it in a high-yield savings account where it is accessible but not tempting.

### 4. Use It Only for Emergencies
An emergency is not a sale at your favorite store. An emergency is a medical bill, a job loss, or a necessary repair. Not a vacation or a new phone.

## Why It Matters

Without an emergency fund, unexpected expenses go on credit cards. Credit cards charge 20%+ interest. One emergency can create a debt spiral that takes years to escape.

With an emergency fund, unexpected expenses are inconvenient — not devastating.

> *Do not save what is left after spending. Spend what is left after saving. — Warren Buffett*
