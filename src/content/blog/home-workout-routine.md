You don't need a gym membership to get in shape. You need a plan that's simple enough to follow on your worst day. Most workout routines fail because they're built for the person you want to be, not the person you are — tired, busy, and not always motivated.

This guide shows you how to build a home workout routine that survives real life.

## The One Rule That Changes Everything

**Never skip twice.** Skipping once is a slip. Skipping twice is the beginning of quitting. Miss a Monday? Fine, do Tuesday. Miss Monday and Tuesday? You're back to square one.

This single rule keeps thousands of people consistent. Your streak can break — it just can't break twice in a row.

## Start Embarrassingly Small

Here's the uncomfortable truth: you're not going to work out for an hour. Not today, not this week. Start with **15 minutes**. That's it.

Fifteen minutes a day, four days a week. After a month, bump it to 20 minutes. After two months, 30. The habit comes first; the intensity comes later.

> **Golden rule:** The best routine is the one you can do on your worst day.

## The 15-Minute Starter Routine (No Equipment)

Do this three to four times a week. No gym, no equipment, no excuses.

1. **Warm-up (2 minutes):** Arm circles, leg swings, marching in place
2. **Squats (1 minute):** As many as you can, good form, slow down if you must
3. **Push-ups (1 minute):** On knees if needed — knees are allowed
4. **Rest (30 seconds)**
5. **Repeat the squat/push-up/rest cycle 3 more times**
6. **Plank (2 x 30 seconds):** Rest 30 seconds between
7. **Cooldown (2 minutes):** Stretch your legs, back, and arms

That's it. Fifteen minutes, roughly 100 squats and 40 push-ups. In two weeks you'll feel the difference.

## How to Make It Stick (The Psychology Part)

Motivation is a liar — it shows up on day one and disappears by day four. What actually works:

### 1. Anchor It to an Existing Habit

Do your workout right after something you already do every day. Wake up → coffee → workout. Get home → change clothes → workout. The anchor reminds you without you thinking about it.

### 2. Prepare the Night Before

Lay out your workout clothes the night before. That sounds silly, but it removes the "I don't feel like changing" excuse — which is the #1 killer of home workouts.

### 3. Make It Public

Tell someone: your partner, your friend, a group chat. "I'm doing 15 minutes today." Accountability works even when you think it doesn't.

### 4. Track Everything

A calendar where you cross off each day is enough. Don't track weight or measurements for the first month — just track **showing up**. Visible streaks are addictive in the best way.

## When to Push Harder

After four to six weeks, your 15 minutes will feel easy. That's the signal to upgrade:

- Add 5 minutes
- Add weights (two water bottles work)
- Do harder variations (full push-ups instead of knee push-ups)
- Add a second round of the circuit

Increase one thing at a time, never everything at once.

## Home Workout Mistakes to Avoid

1. **All intensity, no consistency** — a 10-minute workout done daily beats a 2-hour workout done monthly
2. **Skipping warm-ups** — injuries kill streaks
3. **Comparing to YouTube athletes** — you're not them, and that's fine
4. **Working out hungry or full** — small snack 30 minutes before works best

## The Family Bonus: Work Out Together

You have kids? Make it a family thing. Kids do squats while you do squats. Turn push-ups into a game — "race to 10." Your routine becomes their normal. You're building the habit *and* showing them what consistency looks like.

## Your First Week Plan

- **Monday:** 15-minute routine
- **Tuesday:** rest (or a 20-minute walk)
- **Wednesday:** 15-minute routine
- **Thursday:** rest
- **Friday:** 15-minute routine
- **Weekend:** family walk, hike, or bike ride

Print it, put it on the fridge, and cross off every day. Two weeks from now, you'll be a different person — not because of the exercises, but because you *showed up*.

Start today. Fifteen minutes. No excuses.
