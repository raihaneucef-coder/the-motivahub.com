  - q: "What is the 50/30/20 budget rule?"
    a: "The 50/30/20 rule divides your after-tax income into three buckets: 50% for needs (rent, food, utilities), 30% for wants (entertainment, dining out), and 20% for savings and debt repayment. Popularized by Senator Elizabeth Warren, it's the simplest budgeting framework for beginners."
  - q: "Does the 50/30/20 rule work on a low income?"
    a: "Yes, but percentages may shift. In high-cost-of-living areas, needs often exceed 50%. Adjust to 60/20/20 or 70/10/20 — the principle remains: categorize spending, ensure savings happen first (pay yourself), and keep wants separate from essentials."

    a: "1) Calculate your monthly after-tax income. 2) List all fixed expenses (rent, bills, groceries) — aim for under 50%. 3) Track variable spending for one month. 4) Automate 20% to savings on payday. 5) Whatever remains is your 30% 'wants' budget. Review quarterly."

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-50-30-20_p2">Budgeting does not have to be complicated. The 50/30/20 rule is the simplest, most effective budgeting system for beginners.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-50-30-20_p3">Most people do not budget because they think it is complicated. It is not. The 50/30/20 rule is so simple you can remember it on one hand.</div>


<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-50-30-20_p4"><h2>The Rule</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-50-30-20_p5">- <strong>50% Needs:</strong> Housing, food, transportation, utilities, insurance
- <strong>30% Wants:</strong> Entertainment, dining out, hobbies, shopping
- <strong>20% Savings/Debt:</strong> Emergency fund, <a href="/journal/invest-like-beginner/">investments</a>, debt repayment</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-50-30-20_p6"><h2>How It Works</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-50-30-20_p7"><h3>Example: 3000€ Monthly Income</h3></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-50-30-20_p8">- <strong>50% Needs = 1500€</strong>
  - Rent: 800€
  - Food: 300€
  - Transportation: 200€
  - Utilities: 200€</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-50-30-20_p9">- <strong>30% Wants = 900€</strong>
  - Dining out: 200€
  - Entertainment: 200€
  - Shopping: 200€
  - Hobbies: 300€</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-50-30-20_p10">- <strong>20% Savings = 600€</strong>
  - Emergency fund: 300€
  - Investments: 300€</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-50-30-20_p11"><h2>Why It Works</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-50-30-20_p12"><h3>1. It Is Simple</h3>
No complicated spreadsheets. No tracking every penny. Just three categories.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-50-30-20_p13"><h3>2. It Is Flexible</h3>
The percentages can be adjusted based on your situation. If you live in an expensive city, your needs might be 60%. Adjust accordingly.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-50-30-20_p14"><h3>3. <a href="/journal/fond-urgence/">It Prioritizes Savings</a></h3>
By allocating 20% to savings first, you ensure you are building wealth — not just spending everything you earn.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-50-30-20_p15"><h3>4. It Allows Guilt-Free Spending</h3>
The 30% for wants is your "fun money." You can spend it without guilt, knowing your needs and savings are covered.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-50-30-20_p16"><h2>How to Start</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-50-30-20_p17">1. Calculate your after-tax income
2. Categorize your current spending
3. Adjust to fit the 50/30/20 rule
4. Automate savings transfers
5. Review monthly</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-50-30-20_p18"><blockquote><em>A budget is telling your money where to go instead of wondering where it went. — Dave Ramsey</em></blockquote></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-50-30-20_p21">Where the rule breaks — know its limits: where rent eats more than half of take-home pay, on an irregular income, or under high-interest debt, the 50 slice is fiction, and forcing it kills the plan with resentment. Run the split for one month as a <em>diagnostic</em>, find the category that actually eats your money, then adjust the numbers on purpose. The goal is knowing where your money goes — not worshipping at 50/30/20.</div>
