<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p4">Here is how to build habits that actually last, without burning yourself out.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p5"><h2>1. Start micro</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p6">The most common mistake is aiming too high from day one. You want to run 5 km? Start with five minutes. You want to read 30 pages? Start with one page.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p7"><blockquote><em>A habit is not about intensity — it is about repetition.</em></blockquote></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p8">The brain does not resist change. It resists abrupt change. By starting small you remove the resistance. Five minutes of meditation every morning beats one hour once a month.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p9"><strong>Concrete action:</strong> pick one habit. Shrink it to two minutes maximum. Do it every day for seven days.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p10"><h2>2. Attach it to something that already exists</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p11">The concept is called habit stacking — and it is the strongest way to anchor a new behaviour.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p12">The formula: <strong>After [existing habit], I do [new habit].</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p13">Examples:
<ul><li>After my morning coffee, I write down the three priorities of the day.</li><li>After brushing my teeth, I do ten push-ups.</li><li>After closing my laptop, I walk for ten minutes.</li></ul></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p14">You are not creating a new slot in the day. You are using one that already exists.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p15"><h2>3. Environment beats willpower</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p16">You are not resisting the box of biscuits on the table. You are resisting your forty-seventh attempt to resist it this month.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p17"><strong>Stop relying on your willpower. Change your environment instead.</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p18"><ul><li>Want to eat more fruit? Put it on the counter, not in the fridge.</li><li>Want to read more? Leave the book on the pillow, not on the shelf.</li><li>Want to stop scrolling? Remove the apps from your home screen.</li></ul></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p19">The easiest choice is always the one that appears first. Make the right choice easier to reach than the wrong one.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p20"><h2>4. Never break the chain</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p21">Jerry Seinfeld used a simple method: every day he wrote a joke, he put a red cross on a calendar. After a few days he had a chain. His only goal was <strong>not to break the chain.</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p22">Consistency beats performance. Ten minutes every day beats two hours once a week.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p23">Why? Because the brain learns by repetition, not by intensity. Every day you show up strengthens the circuit. Every day you skip weakens it.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p24"><strong>Tip:</strong> use our <a href="/tracker/">Habit Tracker</a> to see your chain and never break it.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p25"><h2>5. Accept imperfection</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p26">You will miss a day. That is unavoidable. The question is not "will I miss one?" but "what do I do when I miss one?"</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p27">The golden rule: <strong>never miss two days in a row.</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p28">One missed day is an accident. Two missed days are the start of a new habit — the habit of doing nothing.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p29">When you miss:
<ol><li>Write down what happened.</li><li>Adjust it — the time, the length, the place.</li><li>Come back tomorrow, without guilt.</li></ol></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p30"><h2>6. Measure it, and celebrate it</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p31">The brain loves signals of progress. Without measurement you do not see your progress. Without visible progress you lose the motivation to continue.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p32">Every week, ask yourself three questions:
<ul><li>How many days did I hold this week?</li><li>What worked?</li><li>What can I improve?</li></ul></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p33">And when you reach seven days? Celebrate. No feast needed — one line in your journal, one moment of pride. The brain keeps what gets celebrated.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p34"><h2>7. Build an identity, not a goal</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p35">The final mistake: setting goals instead of building an identity.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p36"><ul><li>"I want to run a marathon" → a goal.</li><li>"I am someone who runs" → an identity.</li></ul></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p37">Goals have an expiry date. Identities last a lifetime.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_comment-creer-des-habitudes_p38">Every time you do the habit, tell yourself: <strong>this is what I am.</strong> Not "this is what I do" — "this is what I am."</div>
