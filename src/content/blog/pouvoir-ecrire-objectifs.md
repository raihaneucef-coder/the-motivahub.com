# The Power of Writing Down Your Goals
There is a famous study from Dominican University where students who wrote down their goals were 42% more likely to achieve them than those who did not. Writing is not just recording — it is activating.

When you write a goal, you move it from the abstract to the concrete. It becomes real. It becomes accountable. It becomes yours.

## Why Writing Works

### 1. Clarity
Writing forces you to be specific. You cannot write a vague goal — the act of writing demands clarity.

### 2. Commitment
A written goal is a contract with yourself. It is harder to ignore something you can see on paper.

### 3. Focus
When your goal is written and visible, your brain filters information related to it. You start noticing opportunities you would have missed otherwise.

### 4. Memory
Your brain retains written information better than thoughts. A written goal stays in your mind longer.

## How to Write Your Goals

### 1. Be Specific
"I want to get fit" is weak. "I want to run a 5K in under 25 minutes by December 31" is powerful.

### 2. Use Present Tense
Write as if the goal is already achieved: "I am running a 5K in under 25 minutes." This trains your brain to believe it is possible.

### 3. Make It Visible
Put your written goal where you will see it every day — on your mirror, your desk, your phone wallpaper.

### 4. Review Daily
Read your goal every morning. This keeps it fresh in your mind and reinforces your commitment.

## The Science of Writing

Neuroscience shows that writing activates the reticular activating system (RAS) in your brain. The RAS filters information and directs your attention to what matters. When you write a goal, the RAS starts scanning your environment for opportunities to achieve it.
