Ask most people why they quit something and they will tell you a story about the goal — it stopped mattering, it turned out to be the wrong one, life got in the way. Look closer and a different pattern shows up more often: they quit because they were checking the clock, and the clock kept saying the same discouraging thing.

Progress is rarely linear, but the mind expects it to be. Week one should lead to a slightly better week two, which should lead to a visibly better week three. When the graph refuses to cooperate — when week six looks disappointingly like week one — the temptation is to conclude the method has failed, rather than the more accurate conclusion: the method is working on a timescale the mind wasn't prepared to wait for.

## Compounding Doesn't Announce Itself

Nearly everything that compounds — savings, skill, strength, a body of written work — looks unimpressive for a long stretch before it looks obvious. This is not a design flaw. It is how compounding works: the early curve is flat almost by definition, because there isn't much yet for the growth to act on. The visible acceleration only shows up after a base has been built that nobody could see forming.

The people who benefit from compounding are rarely the most talented ones in the room. They are the ones who kept a boring, invisible process running long enough for the curve to bend. Everyone else concluded, somewhere around month four, that nothing was happening — and they were technically right, and it cost them the part that mattered.

## Measure the Process, Not the Mood

If the outcome refuses to move on a schedule you can see, the honest move is to stop grading each week by the outcome and start grading it by fidelity to the process. Did you show up. Did you do the rep, the page, the deposit. That question has a clean yes or no answer, and it is answerable long before the outcome is.

A visible timeline makes an excellent servant and a terrible judge. Use it to plan. Do not use it to decide, in month three, whether month twenty-four was worth reaching. You cannot see month twenty-four from here — that is exactly why so few people arrive at it.
