We treat sleep as what is left after everything else is done. Work, scroll, one more episode. Then we wonder why willpower is thin by Thursday.

**Sleep is not recovery from the day; it is preparation for the next one.** While you sleep, your brain files what you learned, your body repairs what you stressed, and your emotions reset their baseline. Skip it and you pay with interest — in focus, in patience, in the snacks you did not need.

The advantage is unfair because it is so ordinary. Most people will not protect it, which is exactly why you should:

- **Same wind-down, same time.** Dim lights, no screens, same bedtime. Your nervous system loves rhythm more than intensity.
- **Cool, dark, quiet.** You do not need gadgets; you need the cave your biology expects.
- **Protect the first hour after waking.** No scrolling. Water, light, movement. How you start teaches your brain how to stay.

Try it for seven nights. Not perfectly — just consistently. Notice who you are on day seven versus day one. That person is not more disciplined. That person is simply more rested.

In a world that brags about doing more on less sleep, sleeping well is a quiet rebellion — and a competitive edge no one can copy.
