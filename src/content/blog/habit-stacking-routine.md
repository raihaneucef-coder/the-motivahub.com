# Habit Stacking: The Easiest Way to Build New Routines
You already have dozens of habits. You wake up, brush your teeth, make coffee, check your phone. These behaviors run on autopilot. The key to building new habits is to **attach them to the ones that already exist**.

This is habit stacking — and it is the most reliable way to build consistent routines.

## The Formula

The formula is simple: **After I [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT].**

- After I pour my coffee, I will write my top 3 priorities
- After I brush my teeth, I will do 10 push-ups
- After I sit down at my desk, I will close all tabs except one
- After I eat lunch, I will walk for 10 minutes
- After I get into bed, I will read for 5 minutes

The existing habit acts as a trigger for the new one. You do not need a reminder. You do not need willpower. You just need to follow the chain.

## Why Habit Stacking Works

Habit stacking leverages the brain's existing neural pathways. When you perform a habit, the neural pathway fires. If you attach a new behavior to that firing pathway, the new behavior piggybacks on the existing one.

This is why the order matters. "After I brush my teeth, I will meditate" works. "After I meditate, I will brush my teeth" does not — because you do not meditate every day yet.

Always attach the new habit to an established one.

## Building a Full Routine

You can chain multiple habits together to create a complete routine.

**Morning Routine Example:**
1. After I wake up, I drink a glass of water
2. After I drink water, I do 5 minutes of stretching
3. After I stretch, I write in my journal for 5 minutes
4. After I journal, I review my priorities for the day
5. After I review my priorities, I start my most important task

Each step triggers the next. The entire routine runs automatically.

**Evening Routine Example:**
1. After I eat dinner, I clean the kitchen
2. After I clean the kitchen, I prepare my clothes for tomorrow
3. After I prepare my clothes, I read for 15 minutes
4. After I read, I write down 3 things I am grateful for
5. After I write, I turn off all screens

## Tips for Success

### 1. Start With One Stack
Do not try to build a 10-step routine overnight. Start with one chain: one existing habit plus one new habit. Once that is automatic, add the next link.

### 2. Be Specific
"After lunch, I will exercise" is vague. "After I eat lunch, I will walk around the block three times" is specific. Specificity creates clarity. Clarity creates action.

### 3. Keep It Small
Each new habit in the chain should be small — ideally 2 minutes or less. You can expand later. First, build the chain.

### 4. Never Break the Chain
The power of habit stacking is in the repetition. If you miss one link, the chain breaks and you have to rebuild. Protect the chain at all costs.

## Real-World Application

**For Health:**
- After I wake up, I drink water
- After I drink water, I take my vitamins
- After I take vitamins, I eat a healthy breakfast

**For Productivity:**
- After I sit at my desk, I write my top priority
- After I write my priority, I set a timer for 90 minutes
- After I set the timer, I work without interruption

**For Learning:**
- After I finish work, I read for 15 minutes
- After I read, I write one sentence about what I learned
- After I write, I share it with one person

## The Compound Effect

One habit stacked on another creates a routine. A routine repeated for 30 days becomes automatic. Once automatic, it becomes part of your identity.

You do not have to think about your morning routine. You just do it. That is the power of habit stacking — it transforms conscious effort into unconscious behavior.
