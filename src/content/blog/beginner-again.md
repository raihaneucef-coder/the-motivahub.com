Expertise is a quiet trap.

The longer we stay in one place — same streets, same conversations, same problems wearing new outfits — the more competent we feel and the less curious we become. We stop noticing our own city. Worse, we stop noticing ourselves. Days blur into a routine so smooth it never asks us any questions.

Then you land somewhere unfamiliar, and the world turns the difficulty back on.

You cannot read the menu. You do not know which line is the right one. The simple act of buying bread becomes a small adventure requiring humility, gesture, patience. Within hours of arriving, you have been demoted from expert back to beginner — **and it feels like waking up.**

This is what travel actually offers. Not escape; recalibration.

**Discomfort, chosen in small doses, is the tuition for growth.** At home, we avoid embarrassment at all costs. Abroad, embarrassment becomes hourly — mispronounced words, wrong turns, meals ordered by pointing — and each one passes without catastrophe. You learn something home rarely teaches with such speed: *being bad at things is survivable.* For anyone building anything, that lesson is oxygen.

A few truths worth carrying onto any plane:

- **Go where you know no one.** Comfortable trips confirm who you are; uncomfortable ones expand it.
- **Plan less than feels safe.** The gap in the itinerary is where the story will come from.
- **Talk to strangers deliberately.** Every place is ordinary to someone; their ordinariness is your education.
- **Keep one habit alive on the road** — twenty push-ups, ten journal lines, morning pages. It tells your identity that travel changed your view, not your discipline.
- **Bring the lesson home.** The trip ends; the recalibrated eyes should not.

And there is the deeper return, the one that shows up weeks later. You come back to your own street and see it briefly as a stranger would — smaller in some ways, precious in others. Problems that looked permanent reveal themselves as local. Habits that felt inevitable reveal themselves as optional. Somewhere out there, millions of people live happily without doing things the way you do them. That knowledge loosens something. It makes room.

You do not need a long trip or an expensive one. A bus to a town whose name you cannot pronounce will do. The requirement was never distance — it is unfamiliarity. Go where your competence means nothing, where every hour asks you to learn.

Beginner again. It is the hungriest you will feel all year.
