<div data-i18n-html="article_langage-corps-confiance_p2">Your body speaks before your mouth opens. Learn the body language techniques that project confidence, authority, and presence.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_langage-corps-confiance_p3">You may have seen the claim that communication is 55% body language, 38% tone of voice and only 7% words. That "7-38-55 rule" is widely over-sold. It traces to Albert Mehrabian's 1967 experiments on how people judge a speaker's feelings when a single word and the tone or facial expression contradict each other (Mehrabian &amp; Ferris, 1967; Mehrabian &amp; Wiener, 1967; summarized in <em>Silent Messages</em>, 1971) — and it was never intended to describe everyday communication as a whole. The defensible version is narrower but still matters: when your words and your body disagree, people believe the body. Your body is already speaking — make sure it is saying what you actually mean.</div>


<div data-i18n-html="article_langage-corps-confiance_p4"><h2>Power Postures</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_langage-corps-confiance_p5"><h3>The Victory Pose</h3>
Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart, hands on your hips, chin slightly raised. Hold for 2 minutes. The original 2010 hormone findings (testosterone up, cortisol down) failed to replicate in later studies, so treat this as a confidence posture, not a biochemical guarantee — many people still feel subjectively more assertive and upright before a high-pressure moment.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_langage-corps-confiance_p6"><h3>The Confident Walk</h3>
Walk with your shoulders back, head up, and arms swinging naturally. Take up space. Do not shrink yourself.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_langage-corps-confiance_p7"><h3>The Handshake</h3>
A firm (not crushing) handshake with eye contact and a genuine smile projects confidence instantly.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_langage-corps-confiance_p8"><h2>Eye Contact</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_langage-corps-confiance_p9">- Hold eye contact for 3-5 seconds, then look away naturally
- When listening, maintain steady eye contact
- When speaking, look at different people in the group
- Avoid staring — it is intimidating, not confident</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_langage-corps-confiance_p10"><h2>Voice</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_langage-corps-confiance_p11">- Speak slowly and clearly
- Lower your pitch slightly
- Pause before answering important questions
- <a href="/journal/trouver-voix/">Eliminate filler words</a> like "um," "uh," "like"</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_langage-corps-confiance_p12"><h2>What to Avoid</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_langage-corps-confiance_p13">- Crossed arms (defensive)
- Fidgeting (nervous)
- Looking at your phone (disrespectful)
- Slouching (unconfident)
- Touching your face (anxious)</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_langage-corps-confiance_p14"><blockquote><em>Your <a href="/journal/identity-precedes-outcome/">body language shapes who you are</a>. — Amy Cuddy</em></blockquote></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_langage-corps-confiance_p17">Be honest about what posture research actually supports. Sitting upright reliably changes two things: how other people read you, and how you subjectively feel — it nudges up felt assertiveness and energy. The dramatic hormonal claim — that a two-minute 'power pose' lowers cortisol and raises testosterone — came from a 2010 study (Carney, Cuddy & Yap) that later researchers failed to replicate, so it is a cautionary tale about overreach, not a fact. The grounded takeaway still holds: the body signals the brain, just more modestly than the pop-psych headline promised.</div>
<div data-i18n-html="article_langage-corps-confiance_p18">Three micro-habits that compound: (1) before entering a room, drop your shoulders away from your ears and exhale; (2) when listening, lean slightly forward and mirror the speaker's open posture; (3) when speaking, let your hands rest visible — palms up at mid-height signals trust without words. Practice all three for one week and notice the shift in how people interrupt you (or don't).</div>
