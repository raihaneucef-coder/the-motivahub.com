<div data-i18n-html="article_consistance-bat-intensite_p2">The best athletes are not the most talented. They are the most consistent. Learn why showing up every day beats showing off occasionally.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_consistance-bat-intensite_p3">Every January, gyms are packed. By February, they are empty. The people who show up intensely for a week and then disappear never achieve anything. The people who show up moderately for a year transform everything.</div>


<div data-i18n-html="article_consistance-bat-intensite_p4"><h2>The Math of Consistency</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_consistance-bat-intensite_p5">- Training 1 hour per day for 365 days = 365 hours per year
- Training 3 hours per day for 30 days then quitting = 90 hours per year</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_consistance-bat-intensite_p6">The "intense" person trains for one month. The "consistent" person trains for twelve. The result is not even close.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_consistance-bat-intensite_p7"><h2>Why Consistency Works</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_consistance-bat-intensite_p8"><h3>1. Habit Formation</h3>
Doing the same action in the same slot — same hour, same trigger, same place — <a href="/journal/systemes-battent-motivation/">moves it from decision to default</a>. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Lally et al. (2010)</a> followed real-world habit builders and found a median of 66 days for a simple daily behaviour to start feeling automatic, with a wide range from 18 to 254. Intensity never gets there, because every two-week break restarts the clock.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_consistance-bat-intensite_p9"><h3>2. Progressive Overload</h3>
Your body adapts to the stress you repeat, not the stress you survive once. Adding a small amount — one rep, two minutes, five hundred metres — every week or two is what compounds into strength, speed or size. Three brutal sessions followed by a fortnight off mostly produces soreness, then deconditioning.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_consistance-bat-intensite_p10"><h3>3. Skill Development</h3>
<a href="/journal/strength-is-a-skill/">Skill is not knowledge; it is a pattern your nervous system lays down</a> through tight, frequent reps. Once a week gives your body time to forget between sessions. Ten minutes daily builds the pattern; one hour a month mostly relearns it from scratch.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_consistance-bat-intensite_p11"><h3>4. Mental Toughness</h3>
Showing up on days you do not feel like it is the training itself. The transfer happens because the identity shifts: you stop negotiating with yourself. After forty honest sessions out of a hundred you did not want, a difficult conversation or a cold email starts to look smaller by comparison.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_consistance-bat-intensite_p12"><h2>How to Be More Consistent</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_consistance-bat-intensite_p13">1. Start small — 10 minutes per day is better than 0
