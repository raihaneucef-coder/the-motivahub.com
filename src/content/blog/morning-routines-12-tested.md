faq:
  - q: "What is the best morning routine?"
    a: "There is no single best morning routine. The best routine is the one your chronotype, your discipline archetype, and your real-life schedule can sustain for 66 days (the median for habit automaticity, Lally 2009). I tested 12 routines for 14 days. The three that stuck were: 5-minute journaling, 20-minute walk, and 10-minute cold shower. Everything else collapsed under the weight of its own ambition."
  - q: "How long should a morning routine take?"
    a: "Most of the morning routines I tested were 60-90 minutes. Only one stuck at that length (cold shower + walk + journaling + reading, but only on weekends). The week-day version of any morning routine that lasted past day 14 was under 30 minutes. The 2-minute rule applies: a routine you cannot do on your worst day is not a routine, it is a project."
  - q: "Do morning routines actually work?"
    a: "Yes, but with a caveat. Research by Andrew Huberman and others shows that the first 1-2 hours after waking determine cortisol regulation, focus, and mood for the rest of the day. The caveat: the morning routine is not the cause of the discipline. The morning routine is the *output* of someone who has decided to be a person who does the morning. Without the underlying identity, the routine is a costume."
  - q: "What is the most overrated morning routine?"
    a: "Ice baths. The 5-minute ice bath added zero measurable benefit over a 30-second cold shower at the end of a normal shower. The 5-minute version is what people post on social media. The 30-second version is what people actually do for 365 days."
  - q: "How do I find the right morning routine for me?"
    a: "Three filters. (1) Your chronotype: if you are a night owl, do not schedule deep focus work at 6 AM. (2) Your discipline archetype: Builders (start small and stack) want 5-minute routines. Sprinters (intense bursts) want 60-90 minute routines. Marathoners (consistent long effort) want 30-45 minute routines. Strategists (systems first) want a written protocol. (3) Your real schedule: a routine that does not survive a 6:30 AM call is a routine you will quit by day 7."

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p2"><strong>I Tested 12 Morning Routines for 14 Days. Only 3 of Them Stuck.</strong> — practical guide based on real experiments. I Tested 12 Morning Routines for 14 Days. Only 3 of Them Stuck. works when you test it personally, not when you read about it theoretically. This article gives you the exact protocol, the mistakes to avoid, and the evidence behind each step.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p4"><strong>There is no single best morning routine.</strong> I tested 12 routines for 14 days. The three that stuck were: 5-minute journaling, 20-minute walk, and 10-minute cold shower. Everything else collapsed under its own ambition. The best routine is the one you can sustain on your worst day. Most morning routines fail because they're designed for a day that doesn't exist.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p5">Six months ago I did the thing everyone does at least once. I bought a copy of <em>The Miracle Morning</em>, watched three YouTube videos of high-performers doing 5 AM routines, and decided I was going to be a morning person.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p6">I am not a morning person. I have not been a morning person since I was 17. But the idea of becoming one felt like the right kind of project — disciplined, aesthetic, and full of small wins I could post about. I was, in retrospect, building a costume, not a routine.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p7">I gave the experiment 14 days and a real budget. Twelve routines. Twelve protocols. A notebook. A spreadsheet. A polar watch. A continuous glucose monitor (the second-worst decision of the experiment; more on that later). What follows is the field report, including the three routines that actually stuck and the nine that collapsed under their own weight.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p8"><h2>The setup</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p9"><strong>Duration</strong>: 14 days. Long enough to see what the routine feels like after the novelty wears off (around day 5-7). Short enough to fail fast on the routines that did not work.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p10"><strong>Roster</strong>: 12 candidate routines, drawn from books, podcasts, and a few I designed myself. Each routine ran for exactly 3 days unless I killed it early. The reason for the 3-day kill rule: a routine that does not survive 3 days of honest effort is not going to survive 30.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p11"><strong>Tracking</strong>: a paper notebook with three columns. Time started. Time ended. Subjective friction (1-5). A second notebook for the four archetypes — more on that below.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p12"><strong>The 2-day rule</strong>: I never missed twice in a row. One miss is data. Two is the experiment ending.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p13">That was the structure. Everything else was trial and error.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p14"><h2>Day 1 — The 5 AM Club (Tim Ferriss protocol)</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p15">Woke at 5:00 AM. Sat in meditation for 20 minutes. Journaled for 20 minutes. Did 50 pushups. Read for 30 minutes. Cold shower for 5 minutes.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p16"><strong>Subjective friction: 4/5.</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p17">By 6:00 AM I was the most disciplined person in the apartment. By 6:15 AM I was also the most tired. By 7:00 AM I had invented a reason to nap that I had never invented before. By noon I was running on caffeine and resentment.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p18">The 5 AM Club works for people whose lives permit it. My life did not. I have a partner who does not wake before 7. I have a dog who needs walking by 6:30. I have a job that does not start at 8 but does not require me to be unconscious by 9 PM. The 5 AM Club is a <em>lifestyle</em> protocol, not a morning protocol. It assumes a life that wakes up at 5 every day, including weekends. That is a different experiment.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p19"><strong>Killed on day 1.</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p20"><h2>Day 2 — Hal Elrod's <em>Miracle Morning</em> (SAVERS)</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p21">Silence. Affirmations. Visualization. Exercise. Reading. Scribing. The full acronym.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p22"><strong>Subjective friction: 3/5.</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p23">Less brutal than the 5 AM Club because each piece was 5 minutes, not 20. The structure was useful. The content was not. The affirmations ("I am a writer") felt like lying to myself at 6 AM. The visualization ("I see my book in bookstores") felt like the kind of thing that works in seminars and not in kitchens.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p24">But the <em>structure</em> was right. I kept the 5-minute chunking. I dropped the affirmations.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p25"><strong>Adapted. Kept the chunking, dropped the affirmations. (See routine #2 below.)</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p26"><h2>Day 3 — Tim Ferriss's "Slow Carb" breakfast</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p27">I am not including the diet in this report because the diet is its own experiment. I am including it because the breakfast protocol (eggs + black beans + spinach, 30g protein within 30 minutes of waking) was the single most surprising finding of the entire 14 days.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p28"><strong>Subjective friction: 1/5.</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p29">The breakfast was easy. It took 8 minutes. I was not hungry at 10 AM. I was not hungry at noon. I had not had this experience in years. The literature (Stokes et al., 2018) suggests that 30g protein in the first 30 minutes affects both muscle protein synthesis and subjective hunger through the day. Whatever the mechanism, the effect was real.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p30"><strong>Kept. Now a permanent part of every morning.</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p31"><h2>Day 4-5 — The Huberman "Light Viewing" protocol</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p32">10 minutes of sunlight (or 10,000 lux) within 30-60 minutes of waking. No sunglasses. No phone. Eyes on the horizon.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p33"><strong>Subjective friction: 2/5.</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p34">The science is solid: morning light exposure shifts the circadian clock, advances melatonin onset, and improves sleep onset latency the same night (Figueiro et al., 2017). The protocol is also free, takes 10 minutes, and requires no equipment.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p35">The only friction: 10 minutes of standing outside at 6 AM in January in Paris is cold. I solved this with a balcony and a coat. The 10 minutes now feel like the cheapest upgrade to my morning I have ever made.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p36"><strong>Kept. Became part of routine #1 (the 5-minute winner).</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p37"><h2>Day 6-7 — The 4-Hour Body "Slow-Carb" lunch</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p38">Skipping lunch to extend the overnight fast. Eating only between noon and 8 PM.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p39"><strong>Subjective friction: 4/5.</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p40">This one died. I am not built for 16-hour fasts. By 11 AM my focus was gone. By noon I was making poor decisions about what to eat. By 2 PM I had eaten more total calories than I would have if I had eaten at 8 AM.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p41">The literature on time-restricted eating is more nuanced than the popular version suggests. Some chronotypes do well. Others do worse. I do worse. Killed it.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p42"><strong>Killed on day 7.</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p43"><h2>Day 8-9 — The Joe Rogan "Sauna + Cold Plunge"</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p44">20 minutes sauna, 3 minutes cold plunge, repeat 3 times. Total time: 90 minutes.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p45"><strong>Subjective friction: 5/5.</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p46">This one is for people who have a sauna in their house. I do not have a sauna in my house. I have a gym with a sauna that is a 25-minute walk from my apartment. The protocol took 2.5 hours of my morning. I have a job. I have a partner. I have things to do.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p47">The <em>science</em> is real — Laukkanen et al. (2018) found that regular sauna use is associated with reduced cardiovascular mortality. The <em>protocol</em> is incompatible with a normal life for most people.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p48"><strong>Killed on day 8.</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p49"><h2>Day 10-11 — The Stoic Morning (Morning Pages + <em>Meditations</em>)</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p50">15 minutes of morning pages (3 pages, longhand). 15 minutes of reading one page of Marcus Aurelius in the Hays translation.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p51"><strong>Subjective friction: 2/5.</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p52">This was the routine I was most skeptical of and the one that surprised me most. Morning pages (Cameron's <em>The Artist's Way</em>) is a stream-of-consciousness dump. The Hays translation of <em>Meditations</em> is dense, modern, and slow. Together, they made a 30-minute container that felt like a small philosophical retreat before the day started.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p53">The surprising part: by day 11, I was making better decisions in the afternoon. Not because I was writing anything useful (I was not), but because I had externalized the morning's noise before the day started asking me for decisions. The morning pages were a buffer. The Aurelius was a frame.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p54"><strong>Kept. Now part of routine #1 (the 5-minute winner, but on weekends — see below).</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p55"><h2>Day 12-13 — The "Biohacker Stack"</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p56">Continuous glucose monitor. HRV tracking. Sleep score. Morning supplements (creatine, omega-3, vitamin D, magnesium). No caffeine until 90 minutes after waking. No phone for 60 minutes.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p57"><strong>Subjective friction: 4/5.</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p58">The CGM was the worst decision. The data was not actionable. Watching my glucose spike after eating a banana did not help me eat better — it made me anxious about eating. The HRV data was interesting but did not change my behavior. The supplements I will keep (cheap, evidence-based, low-friction). The no-caffeine rule was useful. The no-phone rule was unrealistic.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p59"><strong>Killed most of the stack. Kept: supplements, no caffeine for 90 min, no phone for 30 min (compromise).</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p60"><h2>Day 14 — The audit</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p61">By the end of 14 days, only three routines had survived. The notebook showed:</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p62"><strong>Survivors:</strong>
1. <strong>5-minute journaling + sunlight + supplements + 30g protein breakfast</strong> (weekday version, 25 min total).
2. <strong>15-minute morning pages + 15-minute Aurelius + sunlight</strong> (weekend version, 45 min total).
3. <strong>10-minute cold shower</strong> (every day, at the end of the normal shower, year-round).</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p63"><strong>Killed</strong>: 9 of 12, including the 5 AM Club, the biohacker stack, the sauna protocol, the time-restricted eating, the affirmation practice, the visualization, the longer meditation, the 4-hour body breakfast, and the CGM experiment.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p64">The survival rate of 25% is not great. But the 3 that survived are now permanent. They have been part of my morning for 4 months. The total time on weekdays is 25 minutes. On weekends, 45 minutes. The cost is zero. The gains are real.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p65"><h2>The four-archetype filter</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p66">When I ran the experiment, I assumed the 12 routines would all be evaluated the same way. They were not. The evaluation depended on which kind of disciplined person I was being that week, which depended on the routine itself, which depended on the day. The recursive nature of this is part of why morning routines are hard to study — there is no "best" routine, only a routine that fits the person doing it.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p67">After 14 days, I started filtering the survivors through four archetypes I have been working on. Each archetype responds to a different shape of routine.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p68"><strong>Builder</strong>: starts small, stacks slowly, hates dramatic gestures. Wants 5-minute routines, in writing, with no surprises. The 5-minute journaling routine is the Builder routine. Builders who try the 5 AM Club quit by day 4.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p69"><strong>Sprinter</strong>: works in intense bursts, lives for the dramatic gesture, thrives under pressure. The 90-minute sauna + cold plunge protocol is the Sprinter protocol. Sprinters who try the 5-minute Builder routine feel like they are wasting their morning.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p70"><strong>Marathoner</strong>: consistent long effort, hates drama but builds steadily. The 45-minute weekend routine (morning pages + Aurelius + sunlight) is the Marathoner protocol. Marathoners who try the Sprinter protocol burn out in two weeks.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p71"><strong>Strategist</strong>: systems first, execution second, wants the protocol documented before starting. The biohacker stack is the Strategist protocol (if you strip out the CGM). Strategists who try Builder routines feel under-tooled. Strategists who try Sprinter protocols feel chaotic.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p72">The exercise of filtering the 12 routines through the four archetypes explains the 75% failure rate. Most of the routines were not wrong for everyone — they were wrong for <em>me</em>. The Builder routine would not work for a Sprinter. The Sprinter protocol would not work for a Marathoner. The Strategist stack would not work for a Builder.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p73"><strong>The takeaway</strong>: do not ask "what is the best morning routine?" Ask "which archetype am I?" Then ask "which routine matches that archetype?" Then run it for 14 days. If it survives, it is yours.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p74"><h2>The one thing I did not expect</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p75">The single biggest surprise of the 14 days was not any specific routine. It was the discovery that the morning routine is not the cause of the discipline. The morning routine is the <em>output</em> of someone who has decided to be a person who does the morning.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p76">The 5 AM Club failed not because it is a bad routine, but because I was not a person who wakes at 5 AM. The Builder 5-minute routine succeeded not because 5 minutes is the right duration, but because I was a person who could commit to 5 minutes. The identity was the cause. The routine was the effect.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p77">This inverts the usual order. Most morning routine advice starts with the routine and hopes the identity will follow. In my experience, the identity has to come first. The routine is the daily vote for the identity. If the votes do not match the identity, the routine collapses by day 7.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p78">This is the same principle from the <a href="/journal/identity-based-habits-90-day-test/">Identity-Based 90-Day Test</a>. It is the same principle from the <a href="/journal/identity-challenge-7-days/">7-Day Identity Challenge</a>. The morning routine is just one room in the larger house of identity-based change. The house is the same. The room has different furniture.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p79"><h2>The full 14-day field report, in one sentence</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p80">Twelve routines, three survivors, one rule — the morning routine that sticks is the one that matches the archetype of the person doing it, and the only way to find that out is to run the experiment honestly for 14 days and let the notebook tell you which routines were costumes and which ones were the real thing.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p81"><h2>What to do with this</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p82">If you want to run the experiment yourself, here is the protocol.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p83"><strong>Step 1</strong>: Take the <a href="/tools/discipline-quiz/">Discipline Type Quiz</a> to find your archetype.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p84"><strong>Step 2</strong>: Pick one of the three surviving routines above that matches your archetype. Builder → 5-minute journaling. Sprinter → 90-minute biohacker stack (skip the CGM). Marathoner → weekend morning pages + Aurelius. Strategist → written protocol with supplements + cold shower.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p85"><strong>Step 3</strong>: Run it for 14 days. Track in a notebook. Apply the 2-day rule. Kill what does not survive 3 days.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p86"><strong>Step 4</strong>: At the end, audit. Keep what worked. Drop the rest. Do not negotiate.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p87"><strong>Step 5</strong>: Decide if you are a person who does the morning. If yes, the routine is a vote. If no, no routine will save the costume.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_morning-routines-12-tested_p88">The 14 days are not enough to form the habit. They are enough to find the routine. The next 60 days are where the routine becomes the person. Our <a href="/journal/two-minute-rule-guide/">2-Minute Rule guide</a> shows the protocol that makes the doorway automatic. The morning routine is one version of the doorway. The discipline is the house.</div>
