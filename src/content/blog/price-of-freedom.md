faq:
  - q: "What does 'freedom is rented, not owned' mean?"
    a: "Financial and personal freedom has no lump-sum purchase price — it requires daily small payments: saving consistently, maintaining health, investing in skills. Miss the payments and you lose the freedom (like rent). The 'price' is boring consistency, not one heroic act."
  - q: "How do I calculate my freedom number?"
    a: "Multiply your monthly essential expenses by 25 (the 4% rule inverse). If you spend 2,000€/month, you need 600,000€ invested to cover expenses from returns. That's your financial 'rent.' Alternatively, calculate what you save monthly and how many years to reach that number at 7% return."
  - q: "Why is freedom harder to keep than to achieve?"
    a: "Achieving requires one decision; keeping requires ten thousand small ones. After reaching a goal, identity shifts from 'pursuer' to 'maintainer' — and humans adapt to comfort (hedonic treadmill). The solution: make maintenance itself a new system, not something you do only when you feel like it."

<div data-i18n-html="article_price-of-freedom_p2">Freedom is not bought once — it is rented every day with small, boring payments. Miss them and the debt returns.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_price-of-freedom_p3">We imagine freedom as a finish line — a number that, once reached, will finally let us breathe.</div>


<div data-i18n-html="article_price-of-freedom_p4">It is a comforting story. It is also false.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_price-of-freedom_p5"><strong>Freedom is not purchased once; it is rented every day.</strong> The rent is not dramatic. It is a series of small, boring payments made when no one is watching: the budget checked, the impulse delayed, the investment left alone.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_price-of-freedom_p6">Skip the payments and freedom quietly moves out. Not with a dramatic eviction, but with small leaks — subscriptions you forgot, dinners that soothed a hard day, a sale you did not need. Each is small. Together they are the reason the month feels shorter than your will.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_price-of-freedom_p7">The good news is that rent, once automated, becomes invisible. <a href="/journal/regle-50-30-20/">A transfer on payday</a> hurts once. A habit of cooking three nights a week saves more than any windfall. Saying no to one upgrade funds the next year's freedom more than saying yes to overtime.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_price-of-freedom_p10">Behavioral finance gives the daily-rent idea a footing. Research on mental accounting describes a consistent pattern: money that is labelled for a purpose and moved out of reach is less likely to be spent on something else, while money left in the main account tends to get absorbed by whatever is in front of you. The person who pays freedom first each payday is not stronger-willed — they simply removed the decision from the moment.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_price-of-freedom_p8">Try this for thirty days. On payday, <a href="/journal/paycheck-trap/">pay freedom first</a> — a fixed amount moved before you see it. Then live on what remains. You will spend less not because you are disciplined, but because you have removed the choice that requires discipline.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_price-of-freedom_p9">Freedom does not arrive with a celebration. It arrives quietly, on a Tuesday, when you realize you could walk away and choose to stay — because you built the choice, one daily payment at a time.</div>
