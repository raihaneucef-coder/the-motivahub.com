<div data-i18n-html="article_eat-the-frog_p2">A famous productivity proverb (often credited to Mark Twain): eat the frog first thing in the morning. Learn why tackling your hardest task first changes everything.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_eat-the-frog_p3">"If the first thing you do each morning is eat a live frog, you can go through the rest of the day knowing the worst is behind you." — often attributed to Mark Twain</div>


<div data-i18n-html="article_eat-the-frog_p4">The frog is your hardest, most important task — the one you are <a href="/journal/real-reason-you-procrastinate/">most likely to procrastinate on</a>. Eating it first means doing it before anything else.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_eat-the-frog_p5"><h2>Why the Frog Works</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_eat-the-frog_p6"><h3>1. Mornings Are Quiet</h3>
Before email, meetings and messages get a vote, almost nothing competes for your attention. That is the real advantage of the morning — not the popular myth of a finite willpower battery, which did not survive large replications (see FAQ). Do the frog while your attention is still yours, before the day spends it on other people's priorities.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_eat-the-frog_p7"><h3>2. It Creates Momentum</h3>
Completing the hardest task first shifts your reference point. The rest of the day is measured against what you already did, not against what you still owe. In practice: send the pitch email at 7am and the two follow-ups at 10 feel easy; postpone the pitch and the same follow-ups become another thing you are avoiding.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_eat-the-frog_p8"><h3>3. It Eliminates Dread</h3>
An avoided task does not sit quietly on a list. It keeps pulling at your attention between other activities, so you pay its emotional cost many times instead of once. The dread is often bigger than the work: a forty-five minute report can haunt you for three days. Eating the frog closes that loop.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_eat-the-frog_p9"><h3>4. It Proves You Are Capable</h3>
Confidence is built from evidence, not affirmations. Each morning you eat the frog, you collect one more data point that you follow through on what you said you would do. After thirty frogs, you no longer have to argue with yourself about whether you are disciplined — the record has already answered.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_eat-the-frog_p10"><h2>How to Apply It</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_eat-the-frog_p11">1. Identify your frog the night before
2. Do it first thing in the morning
3. Do not check email or social media before it is done
4. Celebrate when it is finished</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_eat-the-frog_p12"><h2>Real-World Frogs</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_eat-the-frog_p13">- Writing a difficult email
- Starting a project you have been postponing
- Having a conversation you have been avoiding
- Studying for an exam
- Exercising when you do not feel like it</div>
