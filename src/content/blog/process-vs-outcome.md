faq:
  - q: "What is the difference between a process goal and an outcome goal?"
    a: "An outcome goal describes the result you want — losing 10 kg, getting promoted, publishing a book. A process goal describes the action you take — a 30-minute walk, one piece of visible work each week, 500 words every morning. The outcome is not fully in your control; the process is. Outcome goals set the direction, process goals are what actually produce the result."
  - q: "What is a performance goal?"
    a: "A performance goal sits between the other two. It is a standard you compare yourself with — your previous time, last quarter's numbers, your own benchmark. It is partly in your control and it is measurable, which makes it the bridge between a distant outcome and a daily action."
  - q: "Should I give up outcome goals?"
    a: "No. Without an outcome, a process is just busywork. Keep the outcome, but move it to the monthly review and put your daily attention on the process. Set the direction once, check the direction once a month, and spend the other twenty-nine days on the actions."
  - q: "How do I turn an outcome goal into a process goal?"
    a: "Ask three questions in order. First: what result do I want? Second: what standard would tell me I am getting there? Third: what single action, done daily, moves that standard? Write the third answer down with a time and a place. If it is not possible on your worst day, shrink it until it is."

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p3">There are two types of goals: outcome goals and process goals. Outcome goals are what you want to achieve. Process goals are what you do every day to get there.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p5"><h2>The Difference</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p6"><strong>Outcome Goal:</strong> "I want to lose 10 kg"
<strong>Process Goal:</strong> "I will eat 1800 calories and exercise 30 minutes daily"</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p7"><strong>Outcome Goal:</strong> "I want to get promoted"
<strong>Process Goal:</strong> "I will deliver my best work every day and ask for feedback weekly"</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p8"><strong>Outcome Goal:</strong> "I want to write a book"
<strong>Process Goal:</strong> "I will write 500 words every morning"</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p9">The outcome is not fully in your control. The process is. And focusing on what you can control produces better results than obsessing over what you cannot.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p20"><h2>Three Types of Goals, Not Two</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p21">Most goal advice hands you two categories: the result you want and the actions you take. Sport psychologists work with three, and the missing one is what makes the whole system hold together.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p22"><h3>Outcome goal</h3>The result you want: the medal, the weight, the promotion, the published book. It lives in the future, and other people, your mood and plain luck all have a vote in it.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p23"><h3>Performance goal</h3>The standard you measure yourself against: your last time, your previous quarter, your own benchmark. It is partly in your control, and it is the bridge between the result you want and the action you take.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p24"><h3>Process goal</h3>The action itself: the session, the page count, the call list, the weekly review. It is fully in your control and it can be done today.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p25">The order matters. The outcome sets the direction. The performance standard tells you whether the direction is working. The process is what you do on a Tuesday morning when you do not feel like doing it.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p26"><h2>Process-Oriented vs Goal-Oriented</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p27">Being goal-oriented is not the problem. Living only for the goal is. The difference shows up in five places:</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p28"><ul><li><strong>What you wake up to:</strong> a distance still to cover, or a repetition you can finish today.</li><li><strong>What you measure:</strong> the result, or whether you showed up.</li><li><strong>What a bad week means:</strong> proof that you are failing, or data about the process.</li><li><strong>Where the reward lands:</strong> once, at the end — or every day, at the end of the session.</li><li><strong>What happens after you win:</strong> goal-oriented people stop, and the weight comes back. Process-oriented people keep the action, so the result keeps compounding.</li></ul></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p10"><h2>Why Process Goals Win</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p11"><h3>1. They Are Actionable</h3>
You can do a process goal right now. An outcome goal might take months or years.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p12"><h3>2. They Build Habits</h3>
Process goals, repeated daily, become habits. And habits are the foundation of all achievement.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p13"><h3>3. They Reduce Anxiety</h3>
Focusing on outcomes creates anxiety — there are too many variables. Focusing on processes creates calm — you know exactly what to do.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p14"><h3>4. They Create Consistency</h3>
Process goals are repeatable. You can do them every day, regardless of external circumstances.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p15"><h2>How to Set Process Goals</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p16"><ol><li>Define your outcome goal.</li><li>Ask: "What daily action will move me toward this outcome?"</li><li>Make the action specific and measurable.</li><li>Commit to doing it every day.</li><li>Track your consistency.</li></ol></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p29"><h2>How to Convert an Outcome Goal into a Process Goal</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p30">Run any outcome through the same three questions until you end up with something you can put in a calendar. Here is the full chain for one real goal:</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p31"><ul><li><strong>Outcome:</strong> lose 10 kg.</li><li><strong>Performance:</strong> reach a weight I can hold for a year — roughly 3 to 4 kg per quarter, not 10 kg in six weeks.</li><li><strong>Process:</strong> a protein portion at every meal, a 30-minute walk after lunch, no phone for the first 30 minutes of the day.</li></ul></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p32">The outcome is a wish. The performance standard is a calculation. The process is a Tuesday.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p33">If the process you wrote down is impossible on your worst day, it is not a process yet. Shrink it until it is — then let it grow back on its own.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p34"><h2>The Five Mistakes That Kill Process Goals</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p35"><ol><li><strong>A process with no outcome attached.</strong> Motion without direction. Check once a month that the daily action still points somewhere you actually want to go.</li><li><strong>Too many processes.</strong> Five new daily habits is a schedule nobody keeps. Start with one.</li><li><strong>An unmeasurable process.</strong> "Work on my health" cannot be ticked off. "Walk 30 minutes" can.</li><li><strong>Ignoring the scoreboard completely.</strong> Process worship is how people stay busy for years and never improve.</li><li><strong>Restarting bigger after a break.</strong> A lost week is answered with one smaller action, not with a new 90-day plan.</li></ol></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p36"><h2>Is the Process Really the Goal?</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p37">Partly — and the line is worth keeping sharp. "The process is the goal" is a correct instruction for your attention and a wrong instruction for your review. Attention belongs on the process. The outcome is where you look once a month, to confirm the process is still pointed at something you want.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_process-vs-outcome_p38">Some outcomes will not obey your process: an illness, a layoff, a market that moved. That is exactly why the process gets the daily attention and the outcome gets the monthly review — not the other way round.</div>
