<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p3">Most habits fail because they start too big. You want to read 30 pages, so you do not read at all. You want to run 5 km, so you do not even put on your shoes. The ambition kills the habit before it starts.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p4">The two-minute rule fixes this. And it is deceptively simple.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p5"><h2>What Is the Two-Minute Rule?</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p6">The rule states: <strong>scale any habit down to two minutes or less.</strong></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p7">- "Read 30 pages" becomes "Read one page"
- "Run 5 km" becomes "Put on running shoes"
- "Meditate for 20 minutes" becomes "Sit and breathe 5 times"
- "Write 1000 words" becomes "Open the document and write one sentence"</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p8">The point is not to do the minimum forever. The point is to <strong>start</strong>. Once you start, continuing is natural. The hardest part of any habit is beginning.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p9"><h2>Why It Works</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p10">Your brain resists big changes. When you commit to running 5 km, your brain sees effort, discomfort, and time. It generates resistance. When you commit to putting on running shoes, your brain sees almost nothing. The resistance is minimal.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p11">This is not about being lazy. It is about understanding how your brain works. The brain is wired to avoid effort. By making the habit tiny, you bypass the resistance entirely.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p12">Once you have started, the habit has momentum. And momentum is everything.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p13"><h2>The Science Behind It</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p14">Neuroscience shows that starting a task activates the same neural pathways as completing it. When you put on your running shoes, your brain begins the "running" sequence. Once activated, it is easier to continue than to stop.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p15">This is called the <strong>Zeigarnik Effect</strong> — the tendency for incomplete tasks to occupy your mind more than completed ones. When you start something, your brain wants to finish it. By starting small, you trigger this effect without triggering resistance.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p16"><h2>How to Apply It</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p17"><h3>1. Identify Your Habit</h3>
Choose one habit you want to build. Just one.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p18"><h3>2. Scale It Down</h3>
Ask yourself: "What is the smallest version of this habit?" Then do that version.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p19"><h3>3. Do It Daily</h3>
The two-minute habit must happen every day. Not most days. Every day. Consistency is more important than duration.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p20"><h3>4. Upgrade Gradually</h3>
After two weeks of the two-minute version, increase it slightly. One page becomes two pages. One push-up becomes five. Five minutes of meditation becomes ten.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p21">The two-minute rule is the entry point. You do not have to stay there.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p22"><h2>Common Mistakes</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p23"><strong>Mistake 1: Staying at two minutes forever.</strong> The two-minute rule is a starting point, not a destination. Use it to build the habit, then expand.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p24"><strong>Mistake 2: Skipping days.</strong> The habit must happen daily, even if it is just two minutes. A two-minute habit done daily beats a one-hour habit done occasionally.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p25"><strong>Mistake 3: Choosing too many habits.</strong> Start with one. Master it. Then add another. Trying to build five habits at once is a recipe for building none.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p26"><h2>Real-World Example</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p27">Want to start a meditation practice?</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p28"><strong>Wrong approach:</strong> "I will meditate for 20 minutes every morning." (Too hard. You will quit in 3 days.)</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p29"><strong>Two-minute approach:</strong> "I will sit on my cushion and breathe 5 times every morning." (So easy you cannot say no.)</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p30">After two weeks, you are sitting on the cushion every day. The behavior is automatic. Now you naturally extend to 5 minutes. Then 10. Then 20. The habit built itself.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p31"><h2>The Compound Effect</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p32">Two minutes per day is 12 hours per year. Those 12 hours of small actions compound into something extraordinary:</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p33">- 2 minutes of reading per day = 15 books per year
- 2 minutes of writing per day = 30,000 words per year
- 2 minutes of exercise per day = a body that moves better</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_regle-deux-minutes_p34">The two-minute rule is not about the two minutes. It is about the person you become by showing up every day.</div>
