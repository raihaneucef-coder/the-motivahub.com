We think boundaries push people away. The opposite is true: unclear boundaries slowly poison closeness, until distance feels safer than honesty.

Resentment is almost always a boundary that was not stated in time.

**A boundary is a instruction manual for how to love you well.** It is not a punishment, not a test, not a way to control another person. It is information: *here is where I end and you begin, here is what keeps me open.*

Say it plainly and early:

- **Name the need without blame.** "I need evenings without phones to feel close" lands better than "you are always on your phone."
- **Make it doable.** A good boundary is a concrete action: "let us not discuss work after 9pm" beats "be more present."
- **Keep it when it is hard.** A boundary you abandon under pressure teaches people that your needs are negotiable. Kindness kept consistently becomes trust.

The people who love you want to love you well. Give them the map.

Love without boundaries is exhaustion. Love with boundaries is sustainable — and therefore real.
