<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p8"><h2>The original meaning of discipline</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p9">The word discipline comes from the Latin <em>disciplina</em>, meaning instruction or teaching. The Latin root is <em>discere</em>, meaning to learn. The original meaning of discipline is the system by which a person learns to become the person they want to be.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p10">The schoolroom and prison models corrupted the meaning. In the schoolroom, discipline became a system of compliance. In the prison, discipline became a system of control. The corruption translated into self-directed practice as: discipline is the system by which a person forces themselves to comply with a rule, regardless of whether the rule serves them.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p11">The corrupted model is the one most people use. The corrupted model produces punishment. The corrupted model says: I will force myself to do this hard thing, regardless of how it feels, regardless of what it costs, regardless of who I become in the process. The corrupted model is the most common cause of discipline systems that fail.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p12">The original model is the one that works. The original model says: I will build a system of self-directed rules that help me become the person I want to be. The hard thing is the cost. The cost serves the person. The person is more important than the work. The work is in service to the person.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p13">The two models look identical from the outside. The difference is in the direction of the work. Discipline builds upward. Punishment tears downward. The direction is everything.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p14"><h2>The three tests</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p15">Three tests to determine if your discipline is discipline or punishment. Apply them honestly. Most people will discover that at least one of their discipline systems is actually punishment.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p16"><strong>Test 1: Direction.</strong> Is the work building something or tearing something down? A 5 AM workout that builds strength, endurance, and identity is discipline. The same 5 AM workout that tears you down because you are punishing yourself for not being fit enough is punishment. The direction of the work is the first test. If the work tears more than it builds, the system is broken.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p17"><strong>Test 2: Identity.</strong> Is the work voting for the person you want to become, or punishing the person you currently are? The 5 AM workout is discipline if it votes for "I am a person who is fit." The same 5 AM workout is punishment if it votes for "I am not good enough as I am, so I have to suffer." The vote is the second test. If the work is a vote against the person you are, the system is broken.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p18"><strong>Test 3: Recovery.</strong> Does the work include rest, nourishment, and kindness, or only more suffering? The discipline that includes a rest day, a 2-day rule, and the 2-minute doorway is discipline. The discipline that has no rest, no recovery, and no kindness is punishment wearing a discipline costume. The recovery is the third test. If the work has no recovery, the system is broken.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p19">Apply the three tests to each of your discipline systems. If all three pass, the system is discipline. If any one fails, the system is punishment. The fix is to address the failing test. The fix is not to do more suffering.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p20"><h2>The moment most people cross the line</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p21">The line between discipline and punishment is crossed slowly, not in a single decision. The crossing happens in three predictable stages.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p22"><strong>Stage 1: The original discipline works.</strong> The first weeks of any discipline system feel right. The work serves the person. The person grows. The system is healthy.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p23"><strong>Stage 2: The discipline becomes a way to feel productive.</strong> The work becomes the metric. The person begins to feel productive only when the work is done. Days without the work feel wasted. The work is no longer a service to the person. The work is the metric by which the person judges themselves.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p24"><strong>Stage 3: The discipline becomes punishment.</strong> The work is no longer a service to the person. The work is the punishment for not being the person. The person has to do the work to feel acceptable. The work has become a tax on being human.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p25">The crossing from stage 1 to stage 3 takes months. It is invisible to the person doing the work. The person believes they are still in stage 1. The person believes the work is still serving them. The work has become punishment, but the work wears the discipline costume.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p26">The recovery is the same as the discipline. The 2-minute version. The 2-day rule. The kindness. The recovery is the test that catches the system before it crosses the line. The recovery is what discipline requires. The system without recovery is punishment.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p28">If one of your discipline systems has crossed the line from discipline to punishment, the recovery is the same as the protocol for the miss. The 2-minute version. The 2-day rule. The kindness.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p29"><strong>The 2-minute version</strong>: The smallest possible version of the work. Not because the work is too big, but because the person is too tired. The 2-minute version is the doorway. The doorway is the recovery.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p30"><strong>The 2-day rule</strong>: Never miss twice in a row. <div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p31"><strong>The kindness</strong>: The recovery includes explicit recognition that the person doing the work is more important than the work being done. The kindness is the test. If the recovery does not include kindness, the system is still punishment. If the recovery includes kindness, the system is discipline again.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p32">The recovery protocol is the same for every system that has crossed the line. The protocol is short. The protocol is small. The protocol is kind.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p33"><h2>The kind version of discipline</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p34">The kind version of discipline includes five specific things. If your discipline system has any of these, it is kind. If it has none, it is punishment.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p35"><strong>Rest days.</strong> The discipline system must include rest days. Days when the work is not done. Days when the person is allowed to be the person without the work. The rest day is the test. If the system has no rest days, it is punishment.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p36"><strong>The 2-day rule.</strong> The discipline system must include the recovery protocol. The 2-day rule is the immune system of the discipline. The 2-day rule is the test. If the system has no 2-day rule, it is punishment.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p38"><strong>The explicit recognition that the person matters more than the work.</strong> The discipline system must include the recognition. The recognition is the test. If the system has no recognition, it is punishment.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p39"><strong>The kindness.</strong> The discipline system must be kind. The kindness is the test. If the system is cruel, it is punishment.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_discipline-vs-punishment_p40">These five are not optional. They are the structure of discipline. A discipline system without any of them is not discipline. It is punishment wearing a discipline costume.</div>
