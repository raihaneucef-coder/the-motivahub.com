<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p2">Stop setting vague goals. The SMART framework — Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound — turns wishes into results.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p3">"I want to be successful" is not a goal. It is a wish. And wishes do not come true — plans do.</div>


<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p4">The SMART framework turns vague wishes into actionable plans. It is the most widely used goal-setting checklist in management writing — not because it is the only valid one, but because it forces specificity into a sentence that would otherwise stay a wish. Its evidence base is strongest for specific, challenging goals, which consistently outperform 'do your best' instructions in Locke and Latham's research.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p5"><h2>What Does SMART Stand For?</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p6"><h3>S — Specific</h3>
"I want to get fit" is vague. "I want to run a 5K in under 25 minutes" is specific. The more specific the goal, the clearer the path.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p7"><h3>M — Measurable</h3>
"I want to read more" is unmeasurable. "I want to read 24 books this year" is measurable. If you cannot measure it, you cannot manage it.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p8"><h3>A — Achievable</h3>
"I want to become a billionaire in a year" is unrealistic. "I want to increase my income by 20% this year" is achievable. Goals should stretch you — not break you.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p9"><h3>R — Relevant</h3>
"I want to learn to play guitar" is a nice goal — but is it relevant to your current priorities? Goals should align with your larger vision.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p10"><h3>T — Time-Bound</h3>
"I want to write a book someday" is a dream. "I want to finish my manuscript by December 31" is time-bound. A deadline creates urgency.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p11"><h2>Examples of SMART Goals</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p12"><strong>Bad goal:</strong> "I want to lose weight"
<strong>SMART goal:</strong> "I want to lose 5 kg in 8 weeks by exercising 4 times per week and eating 500 calories less per day"</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p13"><strong>Bad goal:</strong> "I want to save money"
<strong>SMART goal:</strong> "I want to save 3000€ in 6 months by setting aside 500€ per month and cutting 200€ in unnecessary expenses"</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p14"><strong>Bad goal:</strong> "I want to be more productive"
<strong>SMART goal:</strong> "I want to complete 4 deep work sessions per week for the next 3 months"</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p15"><h2>How to Set SMART Goals</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p16"><h3>1. <a href="/journal/pourquoi-profond-objectifs/">Start With Why</a></h3>
Before setting the goal, ask: "Why does this matter?" If the why is not strong enough, the goal will not survive the first obstacle.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p17"><h3>2. Write It Down</h3>
A goal that <a href="/journal/pouvoir-ecrire-objectifs/">is not written is just a thought</a>. Write your SMART goal somewhere you will see it every day.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p18"><h3>3. Break It Down</h3>
A big SMART goal can be broken into smaller SMART goals. "Write a book" becomes "write 500 words per day for 6 months."</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p19"><h3>4. Review Weekly</h3>
Every week, review your progress. Are you on track? If not, adjust the plan — not the goal.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p20"><h3>5. Celebrate Milestones</h3>
When you reach a milestone, celebrate. The brain learns from rewards. Celebrating reinforces the behavior.</div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p21"><h2>The Compound Effect of SMART Goals</h2></div>

<div data-i18n-html="article_smart-goals_p22">One SMART goal achieved is proof that the system works. Ten SMART goals achieved is a transformed life. The compound effect of clear, structured goals is extraordinary.</div>
