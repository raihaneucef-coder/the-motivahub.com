"I want to be successful" is not a goal. It is a wish. And wishes do not come true — plans do.

The SMART framework transforms vague wishes into actionable plans. It is the most proven goal-setting system in the world — and it works.

## What Does SMART Stand For?

### S — Specific
"I want to get fit" is vague. "I want to run a 5K in under 25 minutes" is specific. The more specific the goal, the clearer the path.

### M — Measurable
"I want to read more" is unmeasurable. "I want to read 24 books this year" is measurable. If you cannot measure it, you cannot manage it.

### A — Achievable
"I want to become a billionaire in a year" is unrealistic. "I want to increase my income by 20% this year" is achievable. Goals should stretch you — not break you.

### R — Relevant
"I want to learn to play guitar" is a nice goal — but is it relevant to your current priorities? Goals should align with your larger vision.

### T — Time-Bound
"I want to write a book someday" is a dream. "I want to finish my manuscript by December 31" is time-bound. A deadline creates urgency.

## Examples of SMART Goals

**Bad goal:** "I want to lose weight"
**SMART goal:** "I want to lose 5 kg in 8 weeks by exercising 4 times per week and eating 500 calories less per day"

**Bad goal:** "I want to save money"
**SMART goal:** "I want to save 3000€ in 6 months by setting aside 500€ per month and cutting 200€ in unnecessary expenses"

**Bad goal:** "I want to be more productive"
**SMART goal:** "I want to complete 4 deep work sessions per week for the next 3 months"

## How to Set SMART Goals

### 1. Start With Why
Before setting the goal, ask: "Why does this matter?" If the why is not strong enough, the goal will not survive the first obstacle.

### 2. Write It Down
A goal that is not written is just a thought. Write your SMART goal somewhere you will see it every day.

### 3. Break It Down
A big SMART goal can be broken into smaller SMART goals. "Write a book" becomes "write 500 words per day for 6 months."

### 4. Review Weekly
Every week, review your progress. Are you on track? If not, adjust the plan — not the goal.

### 5. Celebrate Milestones
When you reach a milestone, celebrate. The brain learns from rewards. Celebrating reinforces the behavior.

## The Compound Effect of SMART Goals

One SMART goal achieved is proof that the system works. Ten SMART goals achieved is a transformed life. The compound effect of clear, structured goals is extraordinary.

> *A goal without a plan is just a wish. A SMART goal with a plan is a destiny.*
